# Thebes

A city builder for phone and desktop browsers, inspired by classic Egyptian city builders. It's a personal hobby project.

## Requirements

Python 3 on your PATH, and Microsoft Edge for the command-line tests.

## Run it locally

    python tests/serve.py 8000 .

Then open http://localhost:8000/.

## Play it

    python tests/serve.py 8000 .

Then open http://localhost:8000/. The title card shows a sample city with a finished small obelisk, a colossus going up and a palace; **New city** starts a fresh one, paused, with 2,000 gold and a road in from the edge. On a device that has never played, the title offers one thing, **Start the lesson** (see below); after that, or with a saved city, the first new city opens with seven picture cards (roads, homes, fertile land, food and workers, where gold comes from, the goals, and what lies ahead). **How to play** in the menu, or **Show the picture cards** at the top of it, shows them again, and picture cards for the new rules (a Large plot, a court demand, plague, a raid) each appear at their first moment. The goals strip under the top bar says what to do next, and **Show me** opens the right card and points at the place.

**Learning as you go.** A new city starts with a guided start: the goal under the top bar marks the card it needs with **Suggested** and rings the spot on the map, and every step also works while paused. **Skip guide** turns it off; **Resume guide** in How to play brings it back. Goals keep coming after the opening (homes to grow, people to reach), first-time troubles become goals, and a home's sheet says why it is not growing, with **Show me** or **View**. Small captions celebrate firsts, goals and Town; one-time tips appear on their first trigger; if you sit idle, Show me glows and then one "Next:" line appears. Once the opening is done, three small **daily goals** arrive each day (no streaks; one free swap), and the city summary lists **achievements**. The **Help** setting chooses Guide and tips, Tips only, or Off.

**The economy (Stage 2).** From Town (150 people) the city grows past House: Townhouses want pottery, and four Townhouses or empty plots in a square on a pleasant street join into a Manor, then a Fine manor (beer and two foods), a Villa (linen) and an Estate (jewellery). Each good has a chain: a clay pit, barley farm, flax farm or gem mine fills a **storehouse**, a workshop next to it turns the raw good into pottery, beer, linen or jewellery, and bazaars sell everything homes use. A storehouse's sheet lets you accept or refuse each good. **Desirability** decides which streets can hold the big homes: gardens, statues, temples and booths make a street nicer, workshops and storage make it worse; placing a building tints the ground it would change and the confirm bar says how many homes it makes nicer or less pleasant, and a home's sheet shows its score and the three biggest causes. The **booth** brings entertainment. At Market town a **trading post** opens trade with Memphis (and Byblos at Great city): for each good choose buy, sell or ignore and a level to keep, and a caravan comes every three months. Milestones run up to Capital (1,000 people); new build cards wear a "New" badge, and the next milestone's cards show greyed with when they unlock. Overlays add goods, entertainment and desirability. **Difficulty** (Peaceful, Relaxed, Normal, Hard, chosen under New city) changes what buildings, roads and trade routes cost, and from Stage 3 how fast fire risk builds and how big raids are.

**Danger (Stage 3).** From Town, buildings slowly build fire risk; a **firehouse** (from Village) whose staff reach a building by road keeps the risk low and puts out a small blaze within a day or two. A blaze no staffed firehouse reaches grows into a fire that burns for 10-15 days and can spread to the buildings beside it (roads are firebreaks); a burned home leaves empty plots that refill, anything else leaves rubble to clear. The **fire risk** overlay and a row on every building's sheet show what is at risk and why. Once the city has had 250 people, **raiders** come every few years: a banner warns three months ahead, names their direction and what they are after, and counts down. At Market town you can draw **walls** (a wall over a road is a gate), build **towers** that shoot at raiders within 4 tiles and a **barracks** that trains soldiers, or pay **tribute**: the sheet shows the price beside what the towers to beat them would cost (the very first raid cannot be bought off). Raiders break through walls, set fire to what they reach and steal a little gold; afterwards a short report says what your defences stopped. Combat is automatic. A temple can **hold a festival** to lift the mood. **Peaceful** has no raids and slower fire risk. Only one critical banner shows at a time (a raid under way, then a fire no firehouse reaches, then a raid warning, then debt), and the game pauses for the first fire and the first raid warning.

**On a phone** at most two rows sit under the top bar: the alert (one line; a raid warning is its countdown, and tapping it opens the raid sheet) and one guidance row that the overlay legend, a tip, a caption and the goal take in turn. Captions missed in a busy moment are listed under Recent in the Goals sheet. `tools/shots.ps1 -Mobile` takes and measures the busy moments, and `tests/ui/mobileBudget.test.js` holds the height budget.

**Score and records.** The city summary scores the city out of 100 on People, Prosperity, Culture and Safety, each with the main reason, and gives a medal (bronze, silver, gold) with the points to the next. Milestone times are compared with your best on the same map and difficulty. **Seal this reign** writes the score into the chronicle on this device (the title card lists your best cities and a stamp for each sealed reign) and the city carries on. **This week's Nile** on the title card is the same map for everyone this week; **Share score** makes a link (friends' honour: a link cannot be checked) and a picture, and **Screenshot mode** in the menu hides the interface until you tap. Records travel with Export.

**Monuments (Stage 4).** From City the Monuments tab holds the **builders' guild**, the **quarry** (on rocky ground) and the **brickworks**, and the monuments: the small and large obelisks at City, the colossus and the small mastaba at Great city, the large mastaba at Capital, and the small and great pyramids once the city's prestige reaches 100 and 300. Place a monument anywhere but the fields (⟳ or R turns a mastaba) and give it a road: each working guild within 12 road tiles sends a crew to the nearest site with room, and quarries and brickworks send stone and bricks to the sites in their reach (a storehouse bridges the gap, and Memphis sells stone from City). It rises course by course while the game runs (at 1× a month is 30 seconds, so a small obelisk takes a minute and a half and the great pyramid about 90 minutes, 30 at 3×); its founding is paid when it is placed and the crews' work as it is done (6.5 gold for each unit laid, 130 a crew a month), never taking the treasury below 0, and a quarry, brickworks or guild that no site needs idles with no wages. A site that stops says why, on the map, in the alert list and on its sheet, with the fix. To build your first: put up a builders' guild and a quarry (on rocky ground) near where it will stand, pick the small obelisk, and read the chips on the confirm bar: each says what the spot has (dry ground, a road, a crew, stone near) or what it still lacks; tap ✓ and play. Tap a site to see its course, crews, materials and pace. A mastaba also wants bricks: a brickworks with a clay pit and a storehouse. **Prestige** (one monument of each kind counts) lifts mood, unlocks the pyramids and is the score's fifth part, Monuments; medals are at 200, 300 and 400 of 500, and shared links from before Stage 4 open as older scoring. The **palace** (from Town) is enlarged through five styles from its sheet. **Plazas** and **sphinxes** make grand approaches, festivals walk the roads in procession, and a finished monument gets a dedication procession. Problem markers stand on their buildings.

**A slower month and a new map (Stage 4.5).** At 1x a game month now lasts 30 seconds (it was 20); everything counted in game days stretches with it, while real-time things such as tips and toasts keep their own timing, and 3x is still there. New cities get one of **six river layouts** picked from the map's seed (the classic river down the west side, one in the east, one through the middle, wide bends, an island, and an oasis); maps from before keep their river, and `?seed=`, `?sample=1` and `?stress=1` stay classic unless you add `?variant=east` (or another name). **Clay** is now ground of its own along the river banks, and a **clay pit** must stand on it; older cities gain clay on their free bank tiles and keep any pit already built. A **bridge** is a road drawn across at most 4 tiles of water in a straight line, at 25 gold a tile (the bar names the run and the price); raiders cross bridges like roads. Draw a road across a wall and each wall tile it crosses becomes a **gate** at 20 gold (the bar names the gates), or pick the **Gate** card to start that. A shared score's link now carries the map layout (`&variant=...`, left out for classic, so older links still work), **Start this seed** builds that layout, and personal bests are kept per seed, difficulty and layout.

**Money hints (Stage 4.5).** The gold chip in the top bar shows the month's forecast ("+120 a month"); it turns amber when the treasury would run out within 6 months, and a tap opens the summary at "Spending more than taxes bring in". Every building card and the confirm bar say "Costs N a month" (upkeep and the wages of a full crew) and what the monthly balance would become. If the treasury would go below 0 within 3 months, a one-time tip names the building that costs the most. A seventh picture card, **Gold**, teaches that homes pay taxes and buildings cost upkeep and wages, with an opening goal ("End a month in profit"). The selling daily goal is gone, and the Town unemployment penalty to mood now phases in over six months. New villagers are shown **walking in** from the road entry to their homes on arrival day (drawing only, at most 12 at once, none under Still).

**More danger (Stage 4.5).** Fires under **Gentle** now show flames that change shape at most once a second in place, with a smoke column fading in place; scorch marks show under every motion level. **Raids** grow 10% a year (up to 1.5 times), top out at 18 raiders, and from the fourth raid come as **two bands from two entries** 35% of the time (the warning names both directions); after the wealth they go for a **granary** (food stolen) and a **home** (a quarter of its people flee). From Town, homes build **sickness** risk when no well reaches them, when they are crowded or when the ground around them is poor; at 100 points a home can fall ill, a cloth hangs over its door, it loses people day by day for 20-40 days and the sickness can spread to neighbouring homes. A staffed **physician** (from Town) cuts the risk, makes a covered illness run out twice as fast and stops it spreading. From Market town, low-tier homes (Hut to House) build **crime** risk, faster while the city's mood is below 45 or more than a tenth of the workforce has no job; a home at 100 points can cause a **theft**, which skims some of the treasury or takes goods from a store, and a staffed **police post** (from Market town) cuts that risk. The **Health** and **Crime** overlays show every home with people in it by band, with a dot where a staffed physician or police post reaches it; each home's sheet has its own row, and alerts, tips, goals and mood rows follow the fire pattern. Each of these takes one worker, **Peaceful** has no raids, sickness or thieves at all, and none of it changes the score.

**Living land (Stage 4.6).** **New city** now offers a map size under the difficulty: Small (48 tiles across, a quick city), Normal (64) and Large (80, room for every monument); rock, clay, raider entries and herds scale with the area, and share links, personal bests and stamps carry the size (`?size=48` or `?size=80` picks one when you test). Maps are more varied: inside each of the six river layouts the seed also draws how far the river meanders, side channels and oxbow lakes, rocky outcrops, dune fields, palm groves and dry wadis. Classic at Normal draws as before; the other five layouts look different from Stage 4.5, so an old link to one of them opens another map. **Clay you can find:** clay is ground of its own, terracotta with fine cracks and dug hollows, in patches 2-3 tiles wide along the banks. A **clay pit** now needs only 2 of its 4 tiles on clay, its card says where clay lies, picking it turns **Ground** on and lands the ghost on the nearest fit, and a refused spot says "Needs 2 of 4 tiles on clay (this spot has 1)" with **Show me**; every building tied to ground or water (farms, orchards, mines, clay pits, the wharf and the Nilometer) refuses in the same words and finds its nearest fit the same way. **Bridges are called out:** the road card and the bar say a road crosses water on a bridge and what it costs, a tip appears the first time a road meets water, and Show me points at the nearest crossing.

**The yearly flood and wildlife (Stage 4.6).** From Town the Nile floods every year in months 7-9 at a seeded level (Low, Fair, Good or High) that scales the harvest of farms on the **floodplain** (fertile land within 3 tiles of water) by 0.6, 1.0, 1.1 or 1.25 (the average year is a small gain, x1.01); nothing is damaged and no one is hurt. Without help the level is known when the water rises; a **Nilometer** (it touches water) reads it 3 months ahead, and the farm sheet, a caption and the summary's food forecast say what it means. The water spreads over the floodplain as a blue sheen (still under Gentle and Still) and drains in month 10. Herds of gazelle and ostrich roam the desert and rocks, and ibis, herons, hippos, crocodiles and leaping fish add life along the river (at most 40 drawn at once, slow under Gentle, still under Still). A **hunting lodge** sends hunters to herds within 12 tiles for **game**, taking a little from each herd a month and leaving a breeding stock, and a **fishing wharf** (its long side on the water) lands **fish**, more in flood months; both are foods and count toward a home's second food. A crocodile may pause a riverside building for a day (never on Peaceful, and it never hurts anyone).

**The court (Stage 4.6).** From Market town, once the trading goal is done, the Pharaoh's court starts to write. **Favour** (0 to 100, starting at 50 and drifting back toward it) is on the court sheet, opened from the city summary, with an icon and a number: at 70 or more a gift comes every 6 months and tribute costs 20% less, at 30 or less raids grow a step and tribute costs 20% more (on Peaceful, where there are no raids, favour only brings gifts). **Requests** for goods, gold, food or soldiers sent away for 6 months arrive from year 2, one every 8-14 months, each with a deadline of 6-12 months; the letter states the amount, the deadline, the reward and what refusing costs, and **Send** works once the stores and the treasury hold enough. **Event cards** (sickness, a festival, a merchant caravan, deserters, a temple gift, a lost herd, a craftsmen's dispute, a flood omen) come every 12-18 months with two choices, each stating its exact effect before you tap. Letters sit in the alert row below a raid and above debt, open as one sheet, queue, never pause the game, and past ones are listed on the court sheet; the first is one small, easy request. Favour does not change the score.

**Armies (Stage 4.6).** From Market town there are three kinds of unit, one building each: soldiers (the **barracks**), **archers** (the **archery range**) and **chariots** (the **stables**; their horses eat grain from a granary each month, and hungry horses fight at half strength). Each building trains its own units, which guard the nearest threatened entry on their own. Archers hit twice as hard on or beside a wall or tower, chariots hit twice as hard in the open and half as hard near a wall (and cannot pass walls or gates), and soldiers hit harder holding a gate. Raiders vary too: **enemy archers** from the 3rd raid shoot at your towers, **river raiders by boat** from the 5th on maps whose river reaches an edge, and **enemy chariots** from the 6th, fast, and turned back by walls and rocky ground. The warning names the mix ("14 raiders from the east"), tribute prices the whole raid, and the raid sheet says what helps against each kind, read from the same table the fight uses.

**Monuments and styles (Stage 4.6).** Three new monuments: the **great sphinx** (from Great city), the **sun temple** and the **step pyramid** (both from Capital), ten kinds in all. Every monument is offered in three **styles** as you place it: Whitewashed, Painted (teal and ochre) or Gilded (+10% gold and +1 desirability once finished); old monuments keep their classic look. The **Seven wonders** achievement now asks for 7 of the ten kinds.

**Clearer play (Stage 4.6).** **Enter** lays a pending road, wall, plot, plaza or ghost wherever focus is, and Esc takes it back. Play and pause are drawn as pictures, with the pause bars as tall as the play triangle, and on a phone the corner button turns amber with a **Paused** tag while paused. The title card shows the **version** (4.6) and a build stamp, and **What's new** opens the notes of every build so far, each split into New and Fixed. A legibility pass keeps the overlay legend's words under an alert on wide screens, lets the bottom row scroll at large text, and gives enemy chariots a banner. This is world version 7: older cities keep their maps and play on, with favour at 50 and the flood and herds starting at Town.

**Land and logistics (Stage 4.7).** **New maps** (every map but Classic at Normal, which draws as before; old cities keep their land) now always have **clay, a mine site and farmland near the start** (at least 2 clay-pit fits, 1 mine fit and 6 farm fits within 20 tiles at Normal, repaired by the generator when a draw misses; 200 seeds of every size and layout are tested). **Green belongs to water:** one moisture field (distance to the river, lakes and oases, pushed about by noise) greens the ground along the river and round the **oases** (0-1 on Small, 0-2 on Normal, 1-3 on Large: a pool of 3-8 water tiles with palms, a fertile ring and a little clay, at least 12 tiles from other water), and fades through scrub into sand; open desert gets only drab dry shrubs. **Rock runs in long belts** (2-3 on Normal, 14-40 tiles, grey-brown with strata and boulders, a map always has one) and the **hills** are cliffed ridges on the middle of a belt: nothing passes or builds on them (the Ground overlay and its legend show them, a refusal says "Hills: nothing can be built here", and mines may come to the rocky ground later). Every map stays connected (raiders path round hills by one shared `passable` rule), and **hunters, foot soldiers and archers walk round water and hills** too (a hunter with no way stands at the door). A pool is water to everything but the fishing wharf and the Nilometer, which say "Must touch the river".

**Good spots and the Fetch order (Stage 4.7).** After you place a house the house tool stays on (a **Done** button ends it) and empty plots beside a road are marked from the sim's own coverage: **Good** (water and a bazaar in reach, a bright wash with a drop and a basket) and **Fair** (one of them, hatching with the one icon), with a legend in the guidance row; an overlay hides them while it is on. From the first store a **storehouse or granary can Fetch a good** (Stored, then "Fetch goods"): up to an amount (20 steps to 200, then 100s, default 100), one **cart** per store, 20 units a trip, 8 road tiles a game day each way, from the nearest other store linked by road that holds the good above its own order (so two fetching stores never pass it back and forth), at any distance. The sheet says the consequence before the tap ("Fetch pottery up to 100: one cart, 20 a trip, from the nearest store with pottery, 34 road tiles"), the live status ("20 on the way, back in 3 days") and a refusal in words with Show me; a cut road turns the cart round and a demolished store returns the goods where it can. Carts are drawn on the roads (at most 30; under Gentle they walk like people, under Still they stand at their store). A tip with Show me points at homes whose upgrade waits on a household good held only in a far store.

**Late-game pressure (Stage 4.7).** From **year 8** the capital asks a **yearly tribute** of up to three goods (10% of last year's output on Peaceful and Relaxed, 12.5% on Normal, 15% on Hard, never more than your stores held), warned a year ahead and reminded at 3 and 1 months before the year ends; **Send** on the court sheet ships from any store. Met: favour +5 (+8 by month 6) and a gift of a fifth of its value; missed: favour -15 and the shortfall carried once; it stays on in Peaceful. From **year 10** raids grow with wealth: +1 raider for every 40,000 gold and every 500 people (at most 3, 6 or 9 by level; the warning says "your wealth draws N more") and two-sided raids are likelier; the score ignores it, and Peaceful has none. The **Villa and the Estate** need a third kind of food and more household goods (a Villa of 150 uses 12 pottery, 12 beer and 9 linen a month, an Estate of 180 uses 18, 18, 15 and 11 jewellery), and fall back by the usual two-month countdown if supply breaks: a city saved before 4.7 meets the new need at its next month end. A **Fair flood now gives x1.0** (the average year is x1.0125). This is world version 8: older cities keep their maps and play on, with no hills, oases or green band, empty orders and no carts.

**Know your city (Version 5.0).** A **City advice** button (a scroll) joins the top bar once the first house stands: its sheet ranks the top five things holding the city back (homes stuck for want of a good, a raid on its way, a low street, a shortage coming), each with its cause, the fix, the fix's price and a **Show me** that refuses, saying why, when no tile fits; a dot says the list has changed. Every house sheet starts with the home's **next step** (what to build, where, its price). Three new layers join the overlays: **Home progress** (what each home is missing), **Goods supply** (who asks for a good, and which stores and bazaars hold it) and **Workers** (jobs against jobless). **Build another** on a building's sheet copies it with its style and a store's Fetch orders. **Drag areas** lay farms, gardens and statues like plots, with the total and the skipped tiles shown before you confirm. The road bar has **Route** and **Straight**: Route takes two ends and lays the cheapest road round buildings and hills, over water by a bridge where one is allowed, and prices it first. The camera flings after a quick swipe, **Show me** glides to the place, zoom stays under your fingers and a two-finger double tap zooms out (Gentle shortens these, Still turns them off). Grand homes can be reached by following the advice: goals ask for a third food and a better street, and the advice names the decoration that lifts a low street most. Large maps draw faster (about 1.14 times a Normal map's cost zoomed out, down from 1.77). Old saves load unchanged.

**A harder Nile (Version 6.0).** The late game bites back. Wages, upkeep and monuments cost more with each rank above Town, and from **year 8** the court takes a yearly **due** (a share of the treasury or of a year's taxes, whichever is more; it is cut to a twentieth while the great temple rises, and nothing is taken from a treasury below 0 then), so a rich city can no longer pile up gold: a late city banks about two years of profit. From Town the court also makes **demands** about once a year, for goods, gold, a building or soldiers by a deadline; each states its reward and what failing costs before you accept, an unanswered offer lapses as declined, and favour rises or falls with the answer. Past Capital come two ranks, **Great Capital** (1,250 people and a Villa) and the **Seat of the Two Lands** (1,400 people and an Estate), then the **great temple**: five stages of two courses, four crews, stone and bricks, and an offering of goods as each stage begins. Finishing it **wins the reign** (a closing sheet and a sealed score), and play goes on. **Dangers** come with a warning, a counter, an Advice item and a tip, never in the first three game years (a 5.0 city gets three years from the day it loads), and milder on Relaxed: raiders **wreck** buildings (one tap rebuilds the rubble) and **loot** storehouses, and bands of eight or more follow a **chieftain**; **plague** spreads from a poorly served home and can be held by a physician, a well, room or a paid **quarantine**; a **low Nile** is warned months ahead and halves the farms for a year unless granaries carry the city; **locusts and blight** take the nearest farms, and **Harvest now** takes part of a crop early. Battles can be watched: fighting poses, arcing arrows, the chieftain's banner, burned and wrecked shells, and the result in the alert row (Gentle shortens the motion and Still holds the poses). **Large plots** (a 2x2 card from the first Townhouse) replace merging for grand homes: an empty marked lot shows a Manor checklist and fills as a stocked Manor, and Townhouses on small plots stop growing; homes merged before stay as they are. A **guided first city** is the lesson on a fixed 48-tile map: fourteen steps from the first road to a fire in the bazaar and a raid warning with towers, each lighting its target; replay it from the menu. The title now offers Continue, New city (difficulty and map size under it), Lesson, Settings and What's new, and the map sizes gain **Huge** (112 tiles, best on a tablet or computer). **This week's Nile** is gone. Desktop keys: arrows or WASD, + and −, ] and [, number keys, Esc and right-click are all listed in How to play. Old saves load: a 5.0 city keeps its merged homes, rank and treasury, and its first due waits a year.

**A living world (Version 7.0).** *Trade by river:* **Nubia** (from Capital) and **Punt** (from Great Capital, the only seller of **incense**, which feeds festivals) trade through a new **Quay**, a 2x1 building on a riverbank with a road and four workers (200 gold, from Capital); boats sail in and moor there while caravans still walk to the trading post. The **Trade sheet** (from the trading post, the Quay or the summary's Trade row) lists every route with a switch per good, last year's profit, and **Keep at least** for each good; the great temple's offering and accepted court demands are held back automatically and labelled. On a phone one route is open at a time. *City events:* from Town, once or twice a year (sixteen kinds, half welcome, half troublesome), a card with at most three choices asks for a decision; each choice states its exact consequence first, one you cannot afford is disabled with its reason, an unanswered card takes its stated default, which never costs gold, and a city in debt is never pushed deeper. *Seasons and light:* the land, river, fields and palms show flood, growing and harvest (looks only; farms work as before), the top bar's date names the season, and a six-minute real-time loop brings gentle dusk and night with lamps (Settings → **Day and night**; Still keeps daylight). *Sound:* the music follows the season and turns tense when danger is live, short code-made effects mark placing, roads, fire, battle, caravans and boats, and Settings has separate **Music** and **Effects** volumes under the Sound master. *Advice:* a raid tower is advised only where it truly defends the first target (or the road to it comes first), and goods stranded far from the bazaar that needs them get a storehouse beside it; the Quay, trade floors and a waiting event have items and How to play entries too. *From the 23 Oct playtest:* each **lesson** step now comes up as a card in the middle of the screen and pauses the game until you dock it (Show me, Got it, Enter, Space or Esc), then play resumes by itself when the step is done; **Delete** or **Backspace** demolishes the building whose sheet is open, or takes back the placement an Undo is offered for; **placing** colours each footprint square on its own (with a mark, not only a colour) and a line says every rule that fails, taken from the sim's own check; house plots and building sites that are placed but not yet built are **pegged out** on the map; and on a wide screen an open sheet folds the build tray so the Trade sheet and event card get the panel. Old saves load: a 6.0 city gets every trade switch on, no floors, and its first event no sooner than a year after the load.

**Ambient life and motion.** The river glints, reeds and palms sway, kilns smoke, and now and then a bird or a boat passes. Settings → **Motion** offers Follow the device (the start), Full, Gentle and Still. Full moves everything, the camera glides included; Gentle keeps people, sleds and processions walking while water and smoke fade in place, with nothing swaying, drifting or pulsing and camera moves as cuts; Still moves only people, sleds and processions. Following the device means Gentle on a phone or computer set to reduce motion, and Full otherwise.

**Saving.** The game saves on its own at the end of every game month and whenever you leave the page, keeping the previous save as a backup. Come back and **Continue** opens the Welcome back card: how long you were away, what moved, open goals and alerts; **Carry on** restores your speed. A warning banner appears if saving stops working (private browsing, full storage): export your city from the menu.

**Export and import.** Menu → **Export city** shares a `thebes-city-<date>.json` file (or downloads it); **Import city** loads one. Starting a new city offers to export first.

**Install.** On an iPhone the game shows how to add it to the home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen) before you build anything, because the installed app keeps its own storage; if you built in Safari first, export there and **Bring a city from Safari** in the installed app. Android and desktop Chrome or Edge offer **Install** on the title card and in the menu. Installed, it works offline; when a new version is ready, a toast offers **Restart**. Chat apps' built-in browsers cannot install or keep saves: open the link in Safari or Chrome.

**Sound and vibration** are on by default (the browser starts audio on your first tap; a device that chose otherwise before 6.0 keeps its choice): the Settings sheet, on the title or in the game, turns them off. **Music**, made in the browser as it plays, is on at volume 30 and has its own switch and volume in Settings, as do the short **Effects** (volume 100); Sound is the master of both. The iPhone silent switch is respected.

Add `?seed=7` for another map, `?sample=1` for a small ready-made city with its monuments and palace (seeds 4, 7 and 19 starve; seed 11, the title card's, grows into a Great city), `?stress=1` for a full 64×64 city with three monument sites, `?size=48`, `?size=80` or `?size=112` for a smaller, larger or Huge map, or `?speed=1` to start running.

Keys: Space pauses, ] and [ change the speed, + and − zoom, 1–7 pick a build category, arrows or WASD move the view, M opens the minimap, O cycles the overlays, Enter confirms (whatever has focus), Esc goes back one step (it also closes an open tray), Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z) undoes, Delete or Backspace demolishes the open building (or takes back the placement an Undo is offered for), ? lists the shortcuts. With a mouse: drag to pan, wheel to zoom, click to place. On a phone: one finger pans (a quick swipe flings), two fingers zoom (a two-finger double tap zooms out), tap to select or to move the ghost, then ✓.

To try it on a phone on the same network: `python tests/serve.py 8000 . "" 0.0.0.0`, then open `http://<this computer's address>:8000/` on the phone (saving and installing need HTTPS or localhost, so the phone can play but not install from here).

`tests/screenshot.ps1 -Url '/?skipTitle=1&seed=1&sample=1' -Out shot.png -Width 390 -Height 844` saves a PNG of the page from headless Edge at that size.

## Run the tests

    powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tests/run-tests.ps1

The suite has about 3,700 tests and takes about 9 minutes on mains power, 20 to 30 on a busy or battery-powered machine (use `-WaitSeconds 600`, and run one full suite at a time). The script starts `tests/serve.py` and headless Edge. The test page sends its results back to the server by POST (to `/__results`), and the script waits for them on the real clock for up to 45 seconds (`-WaitSeconds 600` for the full suite), retrying once if they never arrive. It exits 0 only when the output ends with `ALL PASSED`.

Add `-Grep "reach"` to run only tests whose full name contains that text. You can also open http://localhost:8000/tests/ in a browser while the server above is running.

## Tools

`tools/render-assets.ps1` redraws the app icons and `og-image.png` with the art code (commit the PNGs). `tools/shots.ps1 -Tag <name>` takes the before/after screen set at four sizes (`-Stage2` for the Stage 2 scenes, `-Stage3` for the Stage 3 ones: the Peaceful choice, a fire, a raid warning with tribute, a battle at the walls, the score and ambient water; `-Stage4` for the nine Stage 4 scenes under Full and Gentle motion: the title, the picture cards, a pyramid site, a course going up, the capstone, a procession, the palace, a site sheet and the Motion row; all set up by `window.__thebes.devScene`) and `python tools/contact.py <raw dir> <out dir> <prefix> <before tag> <after tag> [--stage2|--stage3|--stage4]` builds contact sheets from it. `tools/tutorial.ps1` makes the picture cards' pictures again from the dev scenes (`assets/tutorial/`, WebP, with the marks' places in `cards.json`); run it whenever the art or the interface's look changes, then `tools/sw-files.ps1`. `tools/sw-files.ps1` rewrites `sw-files.js`, the list of files the offline cache holds, with a build stamp; run it after adding or changing files, before a publish (the test suite fails when it is stale).

For development, `?skipTitle=1` starts a fresh city without the title card, `?welcome=1` starts a sample city behind the Welcome back card, and `?nosw=1` drops the offline cache so edits show without a restart. Neither of the first two writes anything at once, but the next autosave replaces the saved city, so use them on a scratch browser profile.

## Publishing

The site is plain files: GitHub Pages serves `main` from the repository root at `https://joshuahedge.github.io/thebes/`. Every path in the page is relative so it works under that `/thebes/` folder; `.nojekyll` keeps Pages from processing the files.
