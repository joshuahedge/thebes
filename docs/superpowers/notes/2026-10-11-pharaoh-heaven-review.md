# Pharaoh Heaven review: what to borrow for Stage 2 and later

Reviewed 11 Oct 2026 for scoping the next iteration. Source: https://pharaoh.heavengames.com/ (the long-running fan site for Pharaoh, 1999). Everything here is a reading of its guides, charts, FAQs, walkthroughs, interviews and images as **design reference**: mechanics, numbers and player experience. No text, art or data file from the site or the game is reused, and the images were viewed, not kept. The three research digests with the full numbers are in `reference/pharaoh-heaven/` next to this note; the UNVERIFIED marks in them are real (the pages were read through a summarising fetch and some table columns were inferred), so re-check a number on the live page before it becomes game data.

What was read: the nine building tables, VitruviusAIA's six charts (structure sizes, costs by difficulty, desirability radii, fire and collapse risk; housing levels, evolve and devolve thresholds, taxes, entertainment, crime and disease), the Strategy articles (housing levels, entertainment, work camps, production timing, gods, monuments), all four trade-map periods and one commodity sheet, the ten housing-block diagrams and three forum threads behind them, all twelve FAQ categories, eight mission walkthroughs, the download categories, the developer interviews (Rodberg 1999, the Cleopatra and Zeus teams), and sixteen pre-release screenshots.

## 1. The short version

Pharaoh's depth comes from three things: a twenty-level housing ladder gated by services, a walker system that turns every district into a routing puzzle, and scripted missions whose difficulty comes from events rather than size. Our spec already made the right structural choice for a phone game: reach radii instead of walkers, nine tiers instead of twenty, and reasons on every blocked action. The review therefore recommends **borrowing numbers and small rules, not systems**:

1. Desirability as an additive per-tile field with a radius of up to 6 tiles and a table per building (the chart values are a ready calibration).
2. **Hysteresis on housing:** a house drops a tier only when its score falls a few points below the level it needed to rise. Pharaoh does this for every level; we do not, and it is why their cities do not flicker.
3. Entertainment as a tier gate with a points scale (juggler 10, musician 20, dancer 30) rather than a yes/no building.
4. Production as a daily progress bar scaled by staffing, with a monthly yield per building that the balance sheet can reason about (about 47 units a month for a workshop in Pharaoh).
5. A price table and yearly trade quotas per partner per good (1,500 / 2,500 / 4,000 units in Pharaoh) as the calibration for our trading post.
6. A difficulty setting that only multiplies costs and risks (Pharaoh's five levels step costs by about 0.8 and never touch workers or desirability). Cheap, and it gives friends an easy mode.
7. Two late-game ideas worth keeping in the back pocket: top-tier residents who stop working but pay four times the tax ("scribes"), and festivals as a mood lever.

And four things to **not** borrow: walkers with roadblocks, the twenty-level ladder, collapse risk and the architect, and placement rules that need a T-junction.

## 2. The images

Sixteen pre-release screenshots (1999 press shots) and ten player diagrams.

**Screenshots.** The classic look: pale sand with grass tufts, farms as a patchwork of crop rows at different growth stages, stacked whitewashed houses with wooden stairs, a riverside with reed beds, wharves, fishing boats and a sailing barge, a storage yard that shows its contents as stacked amphorae and grain sacks, a monument district with obelisks, sphinxes, columned halls and tiled plazas, and a pyramid under construction with work gangs hauling blocks up a circling ramp. The interface is a right-hand panel: minimap at the top, a grid of hieroglyph build icons, advisors below; the top bar is treasury, population and date. Two lessons for our art, both cheap and both already in the spirit of the spec:

- **Storage should show its stock.** The storage yard reads at a glance because the goods are visible in piles. Our granary and storehouse sprites should carry a fill-level variant (empty, quarter, half, full) once Stage 2 has goods. Same for farms: growth stages make the fields feel alive.
- **Monuments should show the work.** Gangs, ramps and the rising courses are what make a pyramid a goal rather than a progress bar. Stage 4's layer-by-layer build already plans this; keep the figures.

**Diagrams.** The housing-block drawings are the community's answer to the walker system: a ring road around two rows of houses, services on the ring, gardens in the middle, roadblocks at the gaps, industry outside, one spine road to the farms. The 3,000-person "Simon Righarts" block is an interior loop of 2×2 residences with eighteen large statues inside it. What they show is that Pharaoh players spend their effort on **coverage geometry**, and that the geometry is fragile: a loop one tile too long, a cross-road through the block, a dead end, and the far houses silently starve. Our reach model removes that whole class of failure. The price is that our layouts will be less "puzzle"; the spec's desirability and overlays are what should carry the layout fun instead.

## 3. System by system

| System | Pharaoh (from the site) | Our spec | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Housing ladder | 20 levels from Crude Hut (5 people) to Palatial Estate (200 in a 4×4). Each level needs a service: well, food, god, water supply, entertainment, pottery, physician, beer, courthouse, school, dentist + 2 foods, linen, 2 gods, luxury good, mortuary, library + 3 gods, senet house, 2 luxuries, 3 foods. 1×1 up to level 10, then 2×2, 3×3, 4×4. | 9 tiers, 1×1 then 2×2 at tier 6, gates: water, food, religion, pottery (5), figs + beer (7), linen (8), jewellery (9), desirability from tier 6. | Keep 9. Add two gates we lack: **entertainment** (booth) around tier 5 or 6, and treat "two foods" as the spec already does. The 20-level list is a good source of flavour names for our 9. |
| Evolve and devolve | Rise at desirability X, fall only below X minus 2 to 8 points. Residents complain before a house regresses (a deliberate softening from Caesar III). | Downgrade one month after stock runs out; no desirability hysteresis. | **Borrow.** Devolve threshold a few points under the evolve threshold, and a "will lose X in N months" warning (we have the timer; add it for desirability). |
| Desirability | Per-tile field, radius up to 6, values add where they overlap. Gardens 3/2/1, small statue 3/2/1, medium statue 10/8/6/4, large statue 14/14/12/12/10, temple 6/6/4/4/2/2, bazaar −2/−1/0/+1/+2/+3 (better near than next to), granary −8/−6/−4/−2, potter −4/−3/−2/−1, quarry −6 down to −1, mines −12 to −16. Houses themselves exert ±: slums −2/−1, estates +5. Multi-tile houses need only one tile to qualify. | "Falls off with distance"; sources listed; tiers 6 to 9 need minimums; the house sheet shows a breakdown. | **Borrow the table shape and the numbers** as the starting calibration in `data/desirability.js`. Keep the breakdown line ("14 of 20: −6 from potter"), which Pharaoh never shows and players had to compute by hand. |
| Walkers and coverage | Roaming walkers serve houses they pass; roadblocks stop them; loops must be under about 44 to 52 tiles; dead ends and cross-roads break service. Destination walkers (carts, buyers) ignore roadblocks. Silent failure is the top player complaint. | Reach along connected roads with a distance; walkers are cosmetic. | **Keep ours.** Do not add roadblocks. Consider one visible honesty feature instead: the water/food overlays already show has/missing; add the same for entertainment and desirability in Stage 2 (the spec lists them). |
| Labour | City-wide pool; a building is staffed only if a recruiter walker passes within 2 tiles of a house. Work camps staff flood farms first and monuments second, so harvest pauses construction. Residents of manors become scribes and leave the workforce. | Priority order (water and food, safety, services, industry, monuments), proportional sharing, stops under 25%. | Keep ours. | Entertainment | Performer points to houses passed: juggler 10, musician 20, dancer 30, senet 40, kept 6 months; plus a city-wide coverage bonus up to 20. Venues must sit on a road intersection (players call this fiddly). Schools train performers; a school supplies 8 venues. | Booth at Town; "entertainment overlay" in Stage 2; no points. | **Borrow the points scale, skip the intersection rule and the schools.** Booth gives 10 within reach, a bandstand 20, a pavilion 30 (Stage 2 or later). Entertainment becomes a tier gate like water. |
| Production | 16-day months. A building gains a fixed percentage per day at full staffing: workshops 3% (one unit every 34 days, about 47 a month), clay pit 4%, copper 5%, quarries and gold 6%. Output scales with staffing. Cart pushers carry one unit per trip at 3.4 tiles a day. | Producers make output daily, scaled by staffing, into the nearest storage with space. | Same model. **Borrow the numbers** as the starting yields: grain farm already tuned; set potter, brewery, weaver, jeweller near 45 to 50 units a month at full staff, raw producers higher. Our 30-day month means halving the daily rate. |
| Storage | Granary (food only; barley and flax never go there), storage yard with accept / don't accept / get / empty and fill levels. The developer said the storehouse controls matter more than the markets, and that new players can ignore them. Bazaar has two buyers (food, goods). | Granary and a storehouse in Stage 2; bazaars restock monthly from storage in reach. | **Borrow the minimum:** per-good accept / don't accept on the storehouse, nothing more. "Get" orders are the source of the labour-access bug players hate. |
| Trade | Partners buy and sell fixed yearly quotas per good: 1,500 / 2,500 / 4,000 for bulk goods, 15 / 25 / 40 for stone and weapons. A route has an opening cost. Caravans carry 800 units a trip and leave empty if the yard is below the export level. Prices: pottery 105, beer 140, linen 150, luxury 160, papyrus 165; raw copper 185, wood 170, granite 65. Ra can make exports pay 150% for a year. | Caravan every three months, per-visit cap per good, buy/sell/ignore with a stock level, ±10% price drift, no-arbitrage check. | Keep ours, **calibrate from theirs**: yearly quota per good per partner is a cleaner cap than per-visit, and the price ordering (pottery < beer < linen < jewellery) matches the spec's intent. |
| Religion | Five gods with a patron; temples and shrines; coverage per building (patron temple 375 people); festivals every six months; blessings and curses (floods, trade, storage, plague). | Health, education, crime, tax | Apothecary, physician, dentist, mortuary (needs linen); scribal school and library (need papyrus); police and courthouse; tax collectors need a palace; default tax 9%, complaints when collection coverage is under half. If one is ever added, the physician as a mid-tier gate is the natural pick. |
| Fire and collapse | Fire risk is a per-building counter (workshops 20 a tick at Normal, cottages 25, estates 5); firehouse walkers reduce it. Collapse is a separate counter fixed by architects. Both scale with difficulty. | Fire only, from Town, seeded ignition at 3%, firehouse reduces risk by reach. | Keep ours; the per-type risk table (fire peaks at cottage level, falls for rich housing) is a good shape for `data/fire.js` in Stage 3. No collapse. |
| Monuments | 19 monuments; stone ones need about 240 blocks stockpiled before work starts; bricks 400 in one yard; carpenters build ramps; two work camps per mason guild; construction can stall at 99% with no message. | Layer-by-layer with guild crews, gold spent as work happens, pause, prestige per layer. | Keep ours. Lesson for Stage 4: **every stall must say why** (no stone in reach, no crew, paused). |
| Ratings and missions | Four ratings (Culture, Prosperity, Monument, Kingdom) so a lopsided city shows. Requests from the ruler with three outcomes (on time, late, refused) that branch, some into unwinnable states. Population goals climb 150, 600, 1,000, 1,500, 3,500, 7,500, 12,000 across 24 missions; the first three teach one subsystem each; late difficulty comes from scripted events. | Milestones by population (Village 0 to Capital 1,000) and prestige; missions as data in Stage 5. | For Stage 5: **borrow the ramp shape** (one-subsystem teaching missions first), the request-with-a-deadline as the main event type, and avoid unwinnable branches. Our scale stays about a tenth of theirs. |
| Difficulty | Five levels. Costs step by about 0.8 per level (Very Easy is 0.2 of Normal); fire, collapse and goods consumption scale; workers and desirability never change. | None. | **Borrow:** a difficulty setting that is a cost multiplier and a risk multiplier in `data/`, chosen on the title card for a new city, saved with the world. Small. |
| Interface | Thirteen advisors, overlays by hotkey, a Nilometer forecast pop-up, "for sale" signs that attract immigrants. Complaints: stale advisor numbers (population a month old), fiddly placement, hidden failure modes. | Summary sheet, overlays, alerts with cause and fix, blocked-placement reasons. | Our direction is right. One borrow: the **flood forecast** idea becomes "next month" hints in the summary (harvest due, arrivals expected) if we ever add seasons. |

## 4. What this means for the Stage 2 plan

The spec's Stage 2 list stands (goods chains, tiers 5 to 9 with merging, desirability with gardens and statues, booth, the three overlays, trading post, milestones to Capital). The review adds or sharpens these before the Stage 2 brainstorm:

1. **Desirability data table.** Per-building radius values D1 to D6, additive, in `data/desirability.js`, seeded from the chart (gardens 3/2/1, small statue 3/2/1, temple 6/6/4/4/2/2, bazaar −2/−1/0/1/2/3, workshops −4/−3/−2/−1, granary −8/−6/−4/−2). Houses contribute their own value by tier. The house sheet shows the top three contributors.
2. **Hysteresis.** Each tier has an evolve score and a devolve score 2 to 4 points lower; a house below its devolve score for a full month drops, with a warning line in the sheet beforehand.
3. **Entertainment gate.** The booth gives 10 points within its reach; a tier (likely 6) needs 10. Bandstand (20) can wait for Stage 4 or 5; dancers are out of scope.
4. **Production yields as data.** Daily progress per building type at full staffing, with the monthly yield stated in a comment, tuned so a potter feeds about 12 to 15 tier-5 homes.
5. **Storehouse** with accept / don't accept per good and a visible fill level in the sprite. No get orders.
6. **Trade quotas per year** per partner per good, with the per-visit cap derived from them (quota divided by four visits), and a price table whose ordering is pottery, beer, linen, jewellery.
7. **Difficulty** as two multipliers chosen at New city: Relaxed (costs 0.8, risks 0.5), Normal, Hard (costs 1.25, risks 1.5). Saved in the world.
8. **Art slots** for stock levels on granary and storehouse, and growth stages on farms.
9. **Scribes and festivals** go into the follow-ups as candidates, not into Stage 2.

Not changing: reach instead of walkers, nine tiers, no roadblocks, no collapse, no T-junction placement, no gods.

## 5. Questions for Josh

1. Do you want a difficulty choice at all, or should friends all play one tuned Normal? (Pharaoh's data says a cost and risk multiplier is enough; it is a small addition.)
2. Entertainment as a tier gate adds one more thing a new player must learn. Gate it at tier 6 (first 2×2 home, where desirability also starts to matter) or earlier at tier 5?
3. Scribes at tier 9 (no workers, four times the tax) is a strong late-game lever but changes the labour balance. Worth a spike in Stage 2, or leave for Stage 4 with monuments?
4. Festivals: a gold-for-mood button with a cooldown, Stage 3 (where mood pressure from raids arrives) or never?
