All in `src/data/reign.js` unless named.

### Costs that grow (rulings 640, 641): `COST_GROWTH`

| Rank | wages | upkeep | monuments | court |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Village, Town | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Market town | 1 | 1.05 | 1 | 1.05 |
| City | 1 | 1.08 | 1 | 1.1 |
| Great city | 1.01 | 1.1 | 1 | 1.15 |
| Capital | 1.01 | 1.12 | 1 | 1.25 |
| Great Capital | 1.08 | 1.25 | 1.1 | 1.4 |
| Seat of the Two Lands | 1.1 | 1.3 | 1.15 | 1.5 |

- Why so little up to Capital: the capital run sits at Capital from month 20 and must keep 60-70% of its taxes spent over months 49-72 (it was
  67.5%, wages 42.9 and upkeep 9.9 points of it). Each point of wages or upkeep growth there moves that share; 1.01 wages and 1.12 upkeep take it to
  67.3% (upkeep rounding per building gives back some). The village is a Market town from month 8 and must stay at 68-75% with its fetch order
  (73.7%): upkeep 1.05 there costs it 0.2 points.
- Beyond Capital the growth is real (wages ×1.08-1.1, upkeep ×1.25-1.3, monuments ×1.1-1.15): no older run reaches those ranks.
- Seat first set at wages 1.12, upkeep 1.35, monuments 1.2: the great-work run then fell into debt in years 13-17 while its crews worked (the
  treasury below 0 stops a crew's day), so 1.1 / 1.3 / 1.15.
- Easier levels (641): a multiplier is 1 + (row − 1) × the difficulty's cost (0.8 Relaxed and Peaceful, 1.25 Hard).
- Money stays whole (640): `src/sim/economy.js` rounds each building's upkeep and its staff's wages, so the card's bill is what the building pays.

### The court's due (rulings 642, 643): `COURT_DUE`

- Taken on the first day of each year from year 8 (the yearly tribute's first year), with the tribute's goods. The rank's share of the treasury
  (Capital and beyond 45%) or of a year's taxes at this month's rate (Capital 20%, Great Capital 22%, the Seat 25%), whichever is more.
- Why a share of the treasury: the late run starts year 7 with 54,000 banked and a steady ~13,000 a year of profit, at Capital, 1,090 people,
  the very city the capital run's band pins. Growth by rank alone cannot take a city that only stays at Capital below its starting bank; a share
  of the treasury holds the bank at profit ÷ share (13,000 ÷ 0.45 ≈ 29,000 at steady favour), which is the plan's "about two years of profit".
- Why a floor of a year's taxes: a treasury share alone can never put a city into debt; the floor (20% of 43,000 at Capital) does, once
  favour is low.
- Favour: ×2 at 30 or less, ×0.75 at 70 or more (first set ×0.5 / ×1 / ×2, then ×1.5 at low: the careless run then ended at +1,958, not in debt).
- Halved while the great temple rises (first 0.5, then 0.25: at 0.5 the great-work run went into debt for five years).
- Late run, dues taken: 27,310 (year 8, steady favour), 18,530, then 10,590-13,130 a year at high favour.
- Careless run, dues taken: 60,210 (year 8: low favour after the missed tribute), then 17,330 a year (the taxes floor ×2), so −3,600 at year 9.

### The court's demands (rulings 644-650): `DEMANDS`

- From Town, the first 10 months after the city's first month end at Town (or after the load), then every 10-14 months; one at a time; an
  offer waits 3 months. Deadlines: goods 8-12 months, gold 4-6, a building 12-18, soldiers 4-6.
- Amounts: goods 3-5 months of the city's output, gold 1-2 months of taxes (at most 60% of the treasury), each × the court's scale and the
  difficulty's cost; soldiers 30-50% of the units at home. Rewards: goods 50-80% of their value, gold 25-40% back, 60 a unit, a building 50-80% of
  its price. Favour: met +8-12, failed −15-20, declined −5.
- The late run's player accepts what it can meet (answer policy, ruling 660); 7 met and 7 declined or failed over years 1-15.

### Ranks beyond Capital (rulings 621, 653): `src/data/progression.js`

- Great Capital: 1,250 people and a Villa standing. The Seat of the Two Lands: 1,400 people and an Estate.
- Why: the grand district run holds 1,289-1,352 people (peak 1,352 in year 7) with its first Villa in month 98: it becomes a Great Capital in
  year 9 and never the Seat by itself (1,350 would have made it one the month its Estate came, with the Seat's costs on an unprepared city). The
  great-work run reaches the Seat in year 11 with two new streets (1,424 people).

### The great temple (rulings 652-656): `src/data/monuments.js`, `GREAT_WORK`

- 5×5, four crews, 300 crew-months (ten courses of 540 stone and 60 bricks), founding 8,000 (× monuments' 1.15 at the Seat). First set at 240
  crew-months: won on day 5,972 (year 17), under the plan's 18-20.
- Offerings, one as each stage begins: grain 400, clay 300, linen 200, flax 300, grain 500 (× the difficulty's cost). First set pottery 300, beer
  200, linen 150, jewellery 60, grain 600: pottery and jewellery never rest in the stores of a late city (homes take them as made; the grand
  run's stores held at most 0 pottery and 88 jewellery), so the great temple waited for ever; grain, clay, linen and flax are what such a city
  keeps (most held: 707, 860, 924, 554).
- Merged with Tracks C and B1 (rulings 790, 791): the great-work run's stores held 0 flax at every year's end from 11 to 20, so the fourth
  offering is beer 300 (1,177-1,687 held); and the Seat's books ran near even (the treasury −3,649 in years 16-18, crews idle for want of
  gold), so the due while the great temple rises is a tenth, not a quarter. The reign is then won on day 7,030 (year 20), the treasury never
  below 2,313.
