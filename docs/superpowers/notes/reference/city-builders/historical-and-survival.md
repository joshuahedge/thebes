# City-builder reference: historical and survival builders

- **Scope:** Children of the Nile, Nebuchadnezzar, Banished, Timberborn and Anno 1800 in depth. Then short sections on Anno 117, Against the Storm, Manor Lords, Farthest Frontier, Foundation, Ostriv, Kingdoms and Castles, Patron and Dawn of Man. Last come cross-game notes on teaching, needs/tier communication and complaints to design out. Other agents cover Pharaoh/Caesar/Zeus/Emperor and the phone/cozy games.
- **Date:** read and written 11 Oct 2026, as design reference for Thebes. Thebes is an original, Pharaoh-inspired isometric city builder for phone and desktop browsers.
- **Method:** web search plus reading reviews, developer blogs, wikis and forum threads. Every claim is paraphrased. There is at most one short quote per source. URLs and read dates are in the Sources list at the end.
- **Caveats:** many pages blocked automated reading (PC Gamer, Polygon, RPS, GameSpot, MobyGames). Their points are taken from search-result summaries or from Wikipedia's reception sections, and are marked "(via summary)". Anything from my own memory of the games that no source here confirms is marked **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Correction to the brief:** Timberborn's 1.0 came out on 12 March 2026, not 2025 (Mechanistry press release). Farthest Frontier reached 1.0 on 23 Oct 2025. Foundation reached 1.0 on 31 Jan 2025. Against the Storm came to consoles on 26 June 2025.

## 1. Children of the Nile (Tilted Mill, Nov 2004; Enhanced Edition 2008)

Tilted Mill was founded by ex-Impressions staff, so this is the "Pharaoh team's" next Egypt game. It took a different route: a people simulation in full 3D.

### Visuals
- Full 3D. The camera zooms from the whole city down to one citizen, and the right mouse button rotates it through 360°. Multiplayer.it found it intuitive and praised the zoom range.
- Buildings gain mosaics and decorative touches as they upgrade (GameWatcher). Upgrade state shows on the building itself rather than only in a panel.
- Citizens are visibly busy. Labourers haul obelisks and statues to their sites. Fathers work the fields or crafts, children run errands, mothers shop (GameWatcher; Multiplayer.it).
- Zoom in and you hear citizens' voice lines, which reflect the economic and social state of the town. Families hold grudges, for example about a denied burial, long after the cause is fixed (Multiplayer.it).
- Opinions on the art split. GameWatcher called it lush and lively. Multiplayer.it called it decent but evocative, with clipping bugs such as people walking through floodwater.

### UX
- Game functions use Egyptian symbols (GameWatcher). That adds flavour but makes the icons harder to read.
- There are three stat panels, each on its own button. Population shows housing, satisfaction and problems by social class. Raw materials covers food and building supplies. Prestige comes last (Multiplayer.it).
- Criticised as cramped. The build menus are crowded drop-downs that confuse newcomers. Multiplayer.it lists "non-intuitive interface" as a con.
- Design intent, from a GameSpot preview (via summary): the team wanted players to read the city by watching what people are doing, not by studying charts.
- The 2008 Enhanced Edition reportedly focused on accessibility, feedback and interface tweaks (via summary; specifics UNVERIFIED).

### Game design
- **No money.** Bread is the currency. All bread comes from grain grown by peasant farmers, and the number of farmers is capped by the number and wealth of nobles. All wealth therefore traces back to noble estates (Wikipedia).
- Shopkeepers earn food by selling goods. Servants and entertainers earn food by serving nobles. Government workers are paid from food that scribe tax assessors collect (Wikipedia).
- You don't spend money to build homes. You mark out sites and eligible citizens build their own houses. Placing and demolishing are free; the challenge is getting people to occupy the buildings (GameWatcher).
- Each artisan comes from a farmer, who must then be replaced by a villager. Build too many shops, especially at harvest, and the farms go unstaffed (GameWatcher; GameSpot user review via summary).
- Educated workers (scribes, priests, commanders, overseers) are capped by prestige. You raise prestige with monuments, palace upgrades and steles commemorating your achievements. It falls if a royal dies without a proper tomb. Each new pharaoh must earn reputation again (Wikipedia; GameWatcher).
- Unhappy citizens stop working, protest or leave. Nobles eventually want a family tomb (Wikipedia).

### How it teaches the early game
- Three separate tutorials sit on the main menu. Multiplayer.it recommends them even for veterans, and a QT3 beta tester finished all three before playing.
- An in-game guide is available at any time, with historical background (Multiplayer.it).
- GameWatcher found it easily learned. Multiplayer.it found the curve steeper than average.
- Pacing is the recurring complaint: progress means long waits for bread, bricks or papyrus (MobyGames excerpt; GameSpot user review, via summary).

### Reception
- GameRankings 76%. Computer Gaming World 3/5. PC Zone 77/100. Computer Games Magazine 4/5. GameWatcher 8.0. The Enhanced Edition has 85% positive Steam reviews (about 430).
- **Praise:** a city that feels alive, amusing citizen remarks, the depth of the people sim (GameWatcher; Multiplayer.it).
- **Criticism:** slow and waiting-heavy, a crowded interface, shallow abstract military (Multiplayer.it; QT3).

### For Thebes
- **Borrow:** let the map carry state. Upgraded houses visibly gain decoration, and porters visibly carry goods. Thebes already uses tier art. Make sure each of the nine tiers has one obvious visual "tell" readable at phone zoom.
- **Borrow:** short citizen remarks when you tap a house, such as "We have no beer". They cost little, add charm, and explain unmet needs in plain English.
- **Adapt:** commemorations. A stele or obelisk marks a reached milestone and gives a small permanent bonus. This pairs well with Thebes' Village/Town milestone banners.
- **Adapt:** free or cheap relocation. Phone placement is error-prone, so a cheap "move building" lowers the fear of mistakes.
- **Skip:** the bread-as-currency people sim. It is clever but opaque, and too hard to read in five-minute phone sessions.
- **Skip:** long waits with nothing to do. Every slow resource needs a parallel goal.

## 2. Nebuchadnezzar (Nepos Games, Feb 2021)

A two-person Prague studio's direct Pharaoh-like, set in Mesopotamia. It is the closest modern analogue to Thebes. Updates added freeplay, sandbox and difficulty (1.1, Apr 2021), then fire, crime and disease (1.2, Sep 2021), then 40+ gods and festivals (1.3, Feb 2022). A DLC campaign, *The Adventures of Sargon*, followed in Feb 2023.

### Visuals
- 2D hand-drawn isometric, tagged as such on Steam. The sprite work drew Age of Empires comparisons and praise (WayTooManyGames).
- Animations are stiff. Walkers turn abruptly at corners, and farmers appear to teleport between fields. The "Detailed Zoom" setting zooms in further but adds no detail (KeenGamer).
- Visible delivery workers are the main sign of life. GamingOnLinux enjoyed watching goods move but wished ordinary citizens did more on screen.
- A monument editor lets you set layout, colour scheme and decoration within minimum-size rules. Reviewers liked it as a modern take on Pharaoh's prestige monuments (KeenGamer; Checkpoint via summary; bit-tech via summary).

### UX: the modernisations, and where they backfired
- **Assigned routes instead of roaming walkers.** Market sellers follow distribution routes you set, instead of wandering at random. KeenGamer called this a clear improvement over the older games, where roaming sellers skipped houses.
- **Caravans between warehouses.** You set up routes with a "+" button, and can copy a route through a flag under the caravan. Routes can have several pick-up and drop-off stops (SteamAH guide).
- **Logistics became the job.** Small working radii force many warehouses and routes. Expanding or renovating the city breaks routes, and keeping goods distributed is the most time-consuming task (KeenGamer). WayTooManyGames called it a logistics nightmare.
- **Warehouse rules surprise players.** A production warehouse serves whichever caravan arrives first, and caravans take as much as they can carry. An export caravan can therefore empty stock meant for local markets. Overflow fills warehouses and stops production (SteamAH guide).
- **Information is buried.** There are many sub-menus and key information is spread across them (WayTooManyGames). A resource overview shows production against consumption, but KeenGamer found it inaccurate. The road tool doesn't path around buildings.
- **Later QoL:** UI scale, more tooltips, colour-coded unemployment rate, clickable walkers with voice lines, autosave on mission completion (1.1). An option to make warehouses accept nothing by default (1.1.3). An events manager to choose which events notify you (1.3) (GOG release notes; GamingOnLinux).

### Game design
- The developers describe three basic resources: workforce, goods and money. Each depends on the others in a feedback loop. Houses hold a workforce, the workforce makes goods, and goods upgrade houses (developer quote via SteamAH).
- Several population classes live in different house types with evolution stages. Food deliveries such as bread push houses up (via summary; exact class names UNVERIFIED).
- **The devolution spiral:** if supply lapses, houses downgrade, residents leave, and whole industries stop for lack of workers (SteamAH; KeenGamer).
- Buildings have worker slots by workforce type. A farm has five slots mixing haulers and farmers, each farmer can work only a limited number of fields, and one farm can grow mixed crops (KeenGamer).
- **Gold at launch came only from trade,** which unlocks once the town is a reasonably well-known outpost. WayTooManyGames called this its worst decision and said the economy was painfully slow. Bit-tech (via summary) noted you could run out of gold before trade opens. Taxes and wages arrived in 1.1.
- Maps are small, so layouts need planning in advance (KeenGamer).

### How it teaches the early game
- A campaign of 12 historical missions at launch, now "more than a dozen", or 16 per GOG. The first half works as lessons, the second half opens up (bit-tech via summary).
- Each mission opens with a short history lesson on the period. KeenGamer and Checkpoint both liked this.
- GamingOnLinux said the first levels guide you well but later ones become punishing. WayTooManyGames found the start very slow. An encyclopedia is available (Checkpoint, via summary).
- There was no sandbox at launch, which drew criticism. 1.1 added one.

### Reception
- Metacritic 73. OpenCritic top-critic average 58, with 27% recommending. Steam 79% positive (about 1,450).
- **PC Gamer 64:** has class but not enough fun, dragged down by micromanagement and trading (via summary/Wikipedia).
- **RPS:** too thin at launch. It had temples but no religion, entertainment, war, disease or taxes (via Wikipedia). Later updates added most of these.
- **Eurogamer:** historically accurate but repetitive missions (via Wikipedia).
- **KeenGamer 5/10:** half-finished. **WayTooManyGames 6.5:** bureaucratic and slow.
- **Praise:** presentation, music, the setting, assigned seller routes, the monument editor.

### For Thebes
- **Validation:** Nebuchadnezzar dropped random walkers, and reviewers counted that as a win. Thebes' "reach along roads" goes further in the same direction.
- **Lesson:** don't swap random walkers for manual route management. Players hated routes that break when the city changes. Thebes should route goods automatically; the player only builds roads and buildings.
- **Borrow:** a per-type notification filter, like the events manager. On phone, letting players mute "minor" alerts keeps the single goals strip clean.
- **Borrow:** a short history card at each scenario start, one or two lines in plain English.
- **Design out:** silent house downgrades. Show "this house will drop to Tier 3 in 30 days unless it gets beer", with a "Show me" link. Warn before any workforce loss.
- **Design out:** an early economy with only one money source gated behind reputation. Thebes needs a basic income (taxes) from minute one.
- **Skip for now:** the freeform monument editor. It is a big scope cost. A later "pick one of three monument layouts" would capture some of the appeal.

## 3. Banished (Shining Rock Software, Feb 2014)

A one-person project (Luke Hodorowicz, about 5,500 hours by Dec 2013) and the template for the modern "survival builder".

### Visuals
- 3D on a grid, with a muted, realistic palette. Polygon praised the graphics and animations (via Wikipedia). Game Informer liked the improved look compared with similar games.
- Seasons change how buildings look, through weather and light (Lanoc). Winter snow is the signature image.
- Physical stockpiles of logs, stone, iron, firewood and coal. Their capacity depends on their size, and placing them near producers matters (Banished wiki).
- Crop growth in fields, blossoming orchards, and buildings rising as citizens carry materials in: **UNVERIFIED** (memory).

### UX
- Build menus sit in the bottom-right, opened with F-keys and number keys. Hovering an icon shows details (Lanoc).
- Players pin many windows open at once: the event log, general info, the professions window, and info boxes for individual citizens or buildings (Lanoc; PC Invasion tip via summary).
- **Town Hall window:** one overview of population, education, clothing, health, happiness, workers, workplaces, resource limits and production over time. Its production tab has Limit, Current and Produced columns (Banished wiki).
- **Resource limits:** you cap production of each good so storage doesn't clog. Workers whose good hits its limit become general labourers. The event log warns when storage is full or stock falls below 10% of the limit. Limits need raising as the town grows, or people starve (Banished wiki).
- **Criticism:** Eurogamer found information hard to get, and Polygon flagged weak feedback on player actions (via Wikipedia).

### Game design
- Citizens are the resource. They are born, age, work, have families and die. You assign people to 20 occupations and they work without further direction (Steam page; Wikipedia).
- There is no money and no tech tree: anything can be built once you have the materials. Trade is barter with merchant boats, and boats can bring disease. Nomads ask to join and swell the population (Steam page; Wikipedia).
- Threats are weather, fire, starvation, disease and an ageing population. Land use is a real trade-off, because forests, stone and iron take up space you might want for building (Steam page).
- **Depth versus tedium:** difficulty comes from scarcity (IGN's point, via Wikipedia). As the town grows, survival pressure fades and management takes over (PC Gamer). There is no end goal beyond achievements (Escapist).

### How it teaches the early game
- Four tutorials cover controls, assigning workers, which buildings to use, food production and trade (Lanoc).
- Many players skip them and learn by failing and restarting. Reviewers framed this as acceptable, even satisfying (eip.gg; Lanoc reviewer skipped them).

### Reception
- Metacritic 73. IGN 8.3. GameSpot 8. Game Informer 8. Polygon 7.5. PC Gamer 70. Eurogamer 6. Escapist 3.5/5. Steam 90% positive (about 25,800).
- Eurogamer's summary line: "satisfying, but never spectacular".
- **Praise:** interlocking systems, difficulty, looks. **Criticism:** poor feedback, weak progression, no goals once stable.

### For Thebes
- **Borrow:** a per-good "keep up to N" target, shown as one stepper in the good's panel, with an alert when stock falls under a share of that target. It is simple, and it stops warehouse clutter.
- **Borrow:** seasons that visibly change the map. For Thebes this means the Nile year: flood, growing and harvest seasons, with fields visibly flooding and drying.
- **Adapt:** one summary panel in the Town Hall style, but as a single panel (one panel open at a time), with tabs for People / Goods / Money.
- **Skip:** many pinned windows. That conflicts with "one panel open at a time" and does not work on phone.
- **Design out:** no goals once the town is stable. Thebes' goals strip and milestones already answer this; keep goals coming late in the game.

## 4. Timberborn (Mechanistry; Early Access 15 Sep 2021; 1.0 on 12 Mar 2026)

Beavers in a post-human world, built around water physics, droughts and vertical building. Over a million copies sold by 2023.

### Visuals
- Blocky, voxel-style terrain combined with handcrafted 3D buildings. Game8 scored the visuals 9/10. Water became fully 3D in Update 6 (Oct 2024), and 1.0 brought a visual overhaul (Wikipedia; press release).
- **The weather forecast is colour-coded:** a strip under the calendar shows the coming week as temperate (blue), drought (yellow) or badtide (red) (Timberborn wiki).
- Badwater, the toxic water, looks different from clean water. The view menu adds a water overlay, a storage overlay and a height limit for seeing tall builds (Timberborn wiki).
- Visible construction sites and stock piles: **UNVERIFIED** (memory).

### UX
- **Top-left wellbeing number.** Tap the number to see every need and how many beavers in the district have it met (Timberborn wiki).
- **Inventory strip:** science, materials, ingredients, food, water and badwater. Materials, ingredients and food expand to show storage, production outputs and unused inputs, with satisfied needs in green (Timberborn wiki).
- **Alerts:** each issue type shows a count. Clicking it cycles the camera through the affected buildings. Minor problems deliberately raise no alert (Timberborn wiki).
- **Toolbar** groups with submenus centred above the bar. A journal logs births, deaths and bot events. A population window (F1) covers the whole settlement. It is reportedly called "batch control" in some versions (**UNVERIFIED** name). 1.0 added building duplication.
- **Criticism:** a "massive" two-row toolbar of about two dozen items per row, most not yet usable. Together with a thin tutorial, it left the reviewer unsure what to do next (hamatti.org).

### Game design
- **Wellbeing** sums many needs. Hunger, thirst, sleep and shelter are survival needs. Then come social, fun (split into sub-needs, so varied buildings score more than duplicates), aesthetics (decorations), awe (near monuments), knowledge, spirituality and hygiene (Timberborn wiki).
- Wellbeing milestones raise work speed by 20% per step, up to 260%, plus movement, growth and lifespan bonuses. Reaching 15 as Folktails unlocks the second faction (wiki).
- Food variety has diminishing returns, and guides suggest 3–4 food types (timberborn.org guide).
- **Districts:** each district needs its own housing, water, food and jobs. Distribution posts move goods between them. Guides suggest expanding at around 20–30 beavers (timberborn.org guide).
- **Droughts** last about 1 day on easy, 2–3 on normal and 3–6 on hard, and lengthen each cycle. A warning appears a few hours ahead. Guide rule of thumb: 3 water per beaver per drought day, at least 5 days of food (timberborn.org guide). Badtides (2024) turn sources toxic.
- 1.0 added 20+ automation buildings (sensors, relays, timers). Reviewers felt automation arrives too late (Game8; Gamegeeker).

### How it teaches the early game
- The tutorial covers roughly the first hour, then disappears until the next mission set's conditions are met. Hints often arrive after you've already made a poor choice (Game8, 1.0 review).
- It gives first steps without reasons. It said nothing about drought before the first one, then told the player they had survived it and more would come (hamatti.org).
- 1.0 "reworked" the tutorial (press release). Reviewers still call it weak (Game8; Gamegeeker). One 1.0 review's main complaint was the lack of a campaign or objective mode (via summary).
- Common pattern: players learn through failed runs, then it becomes a relaxing sandbox (hamatti.org).

### Reception
- Metacritic 85. OpenCritic 86, 94% recommend. Steam about 95% positive, with roughly 37,000 reviews in the first days of 1.0.
- **PC Gamer (EA, 2021):** gentler and kinder than most of its genre, and the environmental events are its strongest idea (via summary).
- **Game8 82, Checkpoint 8.5, GameBrief 9.1.** Common criticisms: onboarding, late automation, thin long-term goals once water is stable.

### For Thebes
- **Borrow (strong):** a flood forecast strip. Show next year's Nile flood as low / good / high in three colours, in the top bar. Pharaoh had flood quality but no forecast. A forecast turns a random event into something to plan for, and fits five-minute sessions.
- **Borrow:** a needs breakdown framed as "N of M houses have pottery", in green or amber. It reads in one line and suits the house checklist.
- **Borrow:** one alerts badge with a count, which cycles the camera through affected buildings when tapped. It fits "nothing floating over the middle of the map".
- **Borrow:** a variety bonus with diminishing returns for food. Grain + figs beats grain + more grain, which gives the figs chain a reason to exist.
- **Adapt:** good-panel detail showing stock, made per month and waiting inputs, in the style of Timberborn's expandable inventory.
- **Design out:** a toolbar that shows locked buildings, and a tutorial that ends after an hour. Show only unlocked buildings, plus at most a "next unlock" teaser. Keep the goals strip running all game.

## 5. Anno 1800 (Ubisoft Blue Byte, Apr 2019; Console Edition 16 Mar 2023)

The benchmark for population tiers, needs and production chains.

### Visuals
- 19th-century industrial Victorian setting. Shacknews called it gorgeous. Inverse highlighted smoke, horses and ships making the city feel alive.
- Residents speak through voice lines, text and animated portraits that show their mood (Happiness devblog).
- Industry lowers "attractiveness", and tourists dislike pollution and noise (Wikipedia).
- Shacknews noted that the most efficient grid layouts look the least impressive, so cities end up looking alike.
- **Blueprint mode** places see-through building outlines before you can afford them, then builds each with one click (Wikipedia; Ubisoft tips).
- The Console Edition has a "Postcard View" for framed shots of your city (XboxHub).
- Distinct house models per tier and instant placement without construction stages: **UNVERIFIED** (memory).

### UX
- **UI principles (Anno Union UI devblog, 2018):** the stated aim was to "prioritize functional design over the artistic touch". The always-visible HUD is dark so it doesn't tire the eyes. Pop-ups and notifications are brighter to grab attention. The construction menu aims to build production lines in the fewest clicks, without hunting through windows for which buildings belong to a chain.
- **Residence panel:** shows goods received and unmet needs. The happiness layer shows one of five moods (Angry, Unhappy, Content, Happy, Euphoric) plus a rating of how close the next mood change is (Happiness devblog).
- **Statistics screen:** added in patch 6.0 (Dec 2019), eight months after launch, with five tabs. The production tab compares production and consumption bars, per island or all islands, live with every bonus included. Shaded bar parts show capacity not being used (wiki and Steam threads, via summary).
- **Production ratios:** build-menu tooltips show cycle times, so players work out ratios by hand. One example: a woodcutter and a sawmill both take 15 seconds, so they pair 1:1 (Steam thread via summary). A 2022 complaint: checking a chain's inputs means opening each building separately (via summary).
- **Help gaps:** few icons have hover explanations, and there is no glossary (Shacknews). A 2023 Steam thread complained of no manual and no "how to" tooltips (via summary).

### Console Edition: lessons for touch
- **Radial menus replaced lists,** because list navigation on a controller was slow and tiring (Ubisoft accessibility spotlight).
- **Accessibility fixes:** the selection stays put when the stick returns to centre. Players can cap how many entries a radial menu shows. The cursor is wide, so you don't need precise aim. Text and icons were enlarged for sofa distance, which the team called "quite a battle". Italic text can be turned off (Ubisoft).
- **Trade routes became a step-by-step flow** with checks and guidance, and playtests showed it was less mentally taxing (Ubisoft).
- **Some console changes helped PC too:** owner-coloured minimap regions, and fewer entries per menu category (Ubisoft).
- A left-trigger quick radial holds roads, copy-paste and freely assignable slots (3DJuegos, via summary).
- **Criticism:** menus nest too deeply. Placing a sawmill means opening the build radial, then the Farmer radial, then wood production, then a side list. Each menu style also navigates differently, and notifications hide behind a button (Butwhytho, 7/10). Inverse found the radials intuitive, but the game isn't suited to 30-minute sessions.

### Game design
- **Tiers:** the Old World has Farmers, Workers, Artisans, Engineers and Investors. The New World has Jornaleros and Obreros, and DLC added more (wiki; Wikipedia).
- **Two kinds of need (Happiness devblog):**
  - *Population needs* (basic goods and services) decide how full a house gets and whether it can advance.
  - *Happiness needs* (luxuries) affect only mood. Better mood means fewer riots, more loyalty, small rewards and festivities.
  - Example: farmers need a marketplace to move in at all. Fish and work clothes fill them toward 10 per house. Schnapps and a pub raise happiness but aren't needed for growth.
  - The devs deliberately removed "must be happy to advance", so players can skip luxuries to save space.
- **Needs switch on in stages.** Each need appears only once the tier reaches a population threshold, for example fish for farmers at about 50 farmers and work clothes at about 150 (SegmentNext table; approximate). New demands arrive gradually instead of all at once.
- **Upgrade rule:** the house must be full and every listed need met, with road links to the supplying services. Upgrading adds 10 slots, up to 20 (PCGamesN; guides via summary).
- **Workforce by tier:** factories need workers of a specific tier. A potato farm needs 20 farmers, a steelworks 200 workers (PC Gamer guide via summary). You must keep lower tiers around, because upgrading everyone starves raw production (Ubisoft tips).
- **Islands:** each island has its own fertilities, so no island grows everything, which forces trade routes. Happiness and needs are tracked per island and per tier (devblog; PCGamesN).

### How it teaches the early game
- The story campaign is the tutorial: the player's sister guides the first city. Shacknews felt the guidance then stops and the game assumes you understand everything.
- PC Gamer (via summary) found the opening relaxing and the later stages demanding.
- Console reviewers recommend the campaign to start, with quests guiding you (XboxHub; Butwhytho). They also recommend the Annopedia encyclopedia in the pause menu (XboxHub).
- The gradual needs unlock (above) is itself a teaching device. You meet one new demand at a time.

### Reception
- Metacritic 81 (PC), 83 (PS5). OpenCritic 95% recommend. PC Gamer 84: the grandest and deepest Anno. IGN 8.1. GameSpot 7, headlined "The Prettiest Spreadsheet". Shacknews 8. GameStar 90. Fastest-selling Anno at launch.
- **Praise:** looks, depth, the sandbox. **Criticism:** thin help, cities that look the same, spreadsheet feel.
- **Console:** Butwhytho 7 (clunky controls, depth kept). XboxHub 4/5. Inverse positive.

### For Thebes
- **Borrow (strong):** split "grow" needs from "rise" needs. Basic needs (water, food, a temple in reach) fill a house to its cap. Tier needs (pottery, beer, linen) unlock the next tier. Luxuries only raise mood or tax. Make the split visible in the house panel as two short lists.
- **Borrow (strong):** staged needs. A need appears only when it becomes relevant, for example "Your houses now want pottery" once you have N Tier-3 houses. This is perfect for the goals strip.
- **Borrow:** a dark, quiet HUD with bright attention elements only, and one good-per-month balance bar per good (made vs used). Ship it at launch, not eight months later.
- **Borrow from console:** wide touch targets, few entries per category, a step-by-step trade caravan setup, and readable type at arm's length. A phone is closer to a sofa than a desk.
- **Design out:** deeply nested menus (more than two levels to reach a building) and icon-only explanations.
- **Skip:** multi-island workforce juggling and per-island statistics. That is too much for Thebes' scale.

## 6. Short entries

### Anno 117: Pax Romana (Ubisoft Mainz, 13 Nov 2025)
- **Category needs:** needs sit in categories such as food, clothing, public services and household goods. Each need contributes a supply value toward a category threshold that rises per tier. Example: Libertus food needs 1 (porridge *or* sardines), Plebeian food needs 3 (devblog "Fulfil needs your way").
- **Partial credit:** a need at 50% gives half its bonus, and several partial needs can combine to reach a threshold. Over-supply gives extra bonuses.
- **The UI shows each category as bars:** green when the minimum is met, gold when exceeded. A house is ready to upgrade when every category bar reaches its line.
- **Eight attributes:** income, population, happiness, health, fire safety, knowledge, belief and prestige.
- **Production buildings now buff or debuff in a radius,** for example a bakery gives +2 income and -2 fire safety. The radius and its effects are shown *during placement*. Hover a top-bar attribute to see which buildings push it up or down (devblog "Attributes & buff buildings").
- **Teaching:** a story campaign that doubles as a long tutorial. Hints appear when the economy stalls, for example a reminder to build a warehouse or connect roads (Player2, via summary). One newcomer only learned that houses must connect to the town hall because a prompt said so (via summary).
- **UI criticism:** a confusing mix of horizontal and vertical tabs, build menus hard to find even hours in (review via summary), clumsy on gamepad (TheSixthAxis, 8/10).
- **For Thebes:**
  - *Borrow:* "any one food counts", with variety earning a bonus.
  - *Borrow:* green/gold bars for "met / more than met".
  - *Borrow:* a placement preview of effects, for example "Kiln: +desirability for workshops, -2 fire safety nearby".
  - *Borrow:* hover or tap a top-bar number to see its causes.

### Against the Storm (Eremite Games, 1.0 Dec 2023; consoles 26 Jun 2025)
- **Roguelite builder.** RPS put a run at about an hour with clear goals (via Wikipedia). The developer designed runs at about two hours, so that runs end before success turns into boredom (Game Developer interview).
- **Win and lose are two bars:** Reputation versus the Queen's Impatience. The fiction turns a timer into a character demanding results (Game Developer).
- **Recipe Summary panel (2022 update):** a control panel rather than a glossary, showing only unlocked recipes.
  - An "ingredient mode" (what uses this) and a "product mode" (what makes this). Click a related good to jump to it.
  - Global and per-building production limits.
  - Recipes missing an ingredient turn red, and a Build button jumps straight to construction (Eremite blog).
- **Interface backlash:** a 2022 interface update made the game less readable and players objected. The hotfix added building *names* to the construction menu, bigger checkboxes and a highlighted speed button. Eremite: "The ability to conveniently control the game is our top priority."
- **Teaching:** two guided tutorial settlements introduce one mechanic at a time, and new elements keep appearing until about the third settlement (Fandomentals; TechRaptor via summary). Moving buildings costs nothing, so experimenting is safe.
- **Console:** a fully redesigned gamepad UI by QLOC. Nintendo World Report (8/10) still found production-chain menus awkward on a controller, and leaned on free pausing.
- **Reception:** Metacritic 91, PC Gamer 91, Eurogamer 5 stars. Robert Yang's design review felt it is "overburdened" with near-identical tier-2 crafting buildings.
- **For Thebes:**
  - *Borrow:* the "what makes this / what uses this" toggle as Thebes' production-chain viewer, one panel with two modes.
  - *Borrow:* names under icons.
  - *Borrow:* scenario goals as a progress bar that fills.
  - *Skip:* the roguelite meta and run-ending timers.
  - *Keep in mind:* the two-bar "goal vs pressure" frame for later raid scenarios.

### Manor Lords (Slavic Magic, Early Access Apr 2024)
- **Visuals:** grid-free burgage plots grow along roads you draw, and buildings orient to the terrain. Seasons are vivid. Settlers physically set up market stalls (Seasoned Gaming; Spawning Pint).
- **Upgrade checklists:**
  - Level 2 needs 4 timber, a well, a church, firewood on sale, two food types and clothing on sale.
  - Level 3 needs a stone church, a tavern with ale, three foods and a garment stall.
  - Some requirements depend on distance, so neighbouring plots can show different gaps (PCGamesN; Gamerguides via summary).
- **UI failure case:** players spent hours stuck because the tick marks for met and unmet requirements were small diamonds, tan versus black. The fix was hovering the upgrade button to see the problem (Steam thread via summary). Approval gates immigration at 50%, and a "homelessness bug" stalled many towns.
- **Reception:** critics about 71 on OpenCritic. Players 84% positive on Steam.
- **For Thebes:**
  - *Borrow:* visible stock on market stalls and houses.
  - *Design out:* requirement states shown only by colour or shape. Use words plus an icon plus colour: "Beer: missing — no tavern in reach".

### Farthest Frontier (Crate Entertainment, 1.0 on 23 Oct 2025)
- **House ladder:** Shelter, Homestead, Large House, Manor (Mansion added in 0.9.4). A popular fan planner colour-codes them yellow, green, blue and purple.
- **Desirability gates tiers.** One 2022 guide puts it at 30 for a Homestead and 65 for a Large House, with town-centre upgrades needing 25 of a given house type (via summary; may have changed in 1.0).
- **Desirability is shown three ways:**
  - a colour heat-map while placing a house;
  - an overlay with radius rings and bonus percentages, plus red rings around bad neighbours;
  - a tooltip on each house's desirability bar listing every bonus and penalty.
- **Upgrades:** an auto-upgrade toggle, plus manual upgrades.
- **1.0** added a 142-point tech tree, bridges and policies. Steam 86% positive. Metacritic 83.
- **For Thebes:**
  - *Borrow:* the desirability heat-map during house placement, and the per-house bonus/penalty list.
  - *Borrow:* an optional "hold this house at its tier" toggle, so players can keep labour tiers.

### Foundation (Polymorph Games, 1.0 on 31 Jan 2025)
- No grid. Zones are painted, and roads emerge from where villagers walk.
- Three Estates (Labour, Clergy, Kingdom) give separate reputation tracks that unlock buildings.
- A critic praised the interface for staying legible as things got complex. Pathfinding and bugs drew criticism (Galaxus; Metacritic via summary).
- **For Thebes:** *skip* grid-free and painted zones, which are imprecise on phone. *Keep in mind:* separate reputation tracks as unlock paths, if Thebes ever adds factions such as priests and nobles.

### Ostriv (Yevheniy, Early Access since 2020)
- Grid-free placement with draggable building corners, so fences and row-house facades line up. Gravel paths form where people walk. You set production targets per output, for example bark versus firewood versus planks (Laura Michet's blog).
- **Frustrations:**
  - Injuries and illness switch on at 200 residents with little warning.
  - Migrants simply won't come until a tavern has beer, and the game doesn't explain this.
  - Some luxury goods exist only to raise tax (Laura Michet).
- **For Thebes:** *Design out* silent switches. When fire, raids or disease turn on at a population threshold, announce them a milestone in advance ("At 400 people, fires become possible — build a fire post").

### Kingdoms and Castles (Lion Shield, 2017)
- Voxel art. A bottom status bar shows population, housing space, idle people, wood, stone, food and gold.
- Clicking the keep gives advice from three advisers: agriculture, city and military. Pop-up menus show what citizens want.
- There is no tutorial. The tax adviser even recommends a change without saying you must first build a treasury (Tech-Gaming, 76%).
- Peaceful mode removes enemies. Players praise it as accessible but say it lacks longevity and goals.
- **For Thebes:**
  - *Borrow:* a single thin status bar at the screen edge, which fits "nothing in the middle".
  - *Borrow:* a "peaceful" option for raids, if raids arrive later.
  - *Design out:* advice that names a fix without naming the prerequisite.

### Patron (Overseer Games, Aug 2021)
- Banished-like, with a "social issues" layer: class strife, immigration, religion, health, safety and loyalty. Each citizen has their own needs and worries. Reviewers called it a harder Banished, and survival through the first winter is the hurdle (Prima; Thumb Culture via summary).
- **For Thebes:** *skip.* Per-citizen issue simulation is too much detail for phone sessions.

### Dawn of Man (Madruga Works, 2019)
- You set work zones with a worker limit for flint, sticks, fishing, hunting and gathering.
- **Knowledge points come from milestones** such as your first fish, and are spent to advance through the ages (TechRaptor preview).
- The 7/10 review found progression opaque. Some unlocks give nothing usable yet. It also hit a tutorial-blocking bug.
- **For Thebes:**
  - *Borrow:* "firsts" as small rewards (first fig harvest, first caravan) feeding the goals strip.
  - *Design out:* unlocks that give the player nothing to do right away.

## 7. Early-game teaching patterns (cross-game)

1. **The campaign as tutorial is the norm** (Anno 1800, Anno 117, Nebuchadnezzar, Against the Storm). It works while a quest keeps telling you the next thing. It fails when guidance stops after chapter one: the Shacknews complaint about Anno 1800, and Game8's about Timberborn. **Thebes:** the goals strip must never run dry. When the scripted early goals end, generate goals from the city's state (next tier, missing need, idle goods).
2. **Separate tutorials get skipped.** That happened with Children of the Nile (3), Banished (4) and Timberborn. Reviewers often skipped them and learned by failing. **Thebes:** teach inside the real game, as Thebes already does. Keep any "tutorial map" optional and short.
3. **Introduce demands in stages** (Anno 1800 and 117 activate needs at tier population thresholds; Against the Storm adds elements over the first three settlements). This is the strongest pattern found. **Thebes:** each new need or system arrives as a goal: "Your Tier-3 homes now want pottery. Show me."
4. **Hints when stalled.** Anno 117 pops a hint when the economy stalls, for example "build a warehouse". The opposite is Timberborn, whose hints arrive after the mistake. **Thebes:** trigger hints on stall signals such as idle workers, a full store or an unmet need for N days. Never hint about something already finished.
5. **Warn before a system turns on.** Timberborn warns hours before a drought and shows a week-ahead forecast. Counter-examples: Ostriv's illness at 200 residents and Timberborn's first drought in the tutorial. **Thebes:** a forecast strip, plus a milestone that announces upcoming threats.
6. **Short scenarios with visible win bars** (Against the Storm) make sessions finite. **Thebes:** each goal shows a small progress bar. A phone session can end on a completed goal.
7. **Encyclopedias are a fallback, not a teacher** (Annopedia, Children of the Nile's guide, Nebuchadnezzar's encyclopedia). Shacknews faulted Anno 1800 for having no glossary at all. **Thebes:** a plain-English "What is this?" line in every panel beats a separate wiki.
8. **Only show what's usable.** Timberborn's toolbar full of locked items overwhelmed a veteran. Against the Storm's recipe panel deliberately shows only unlocked recipes. **Thebes:** hide locked buildings, and show one "unlocks at Town" teaser at most.
9. **Cheap undo lowers fear.** Against the Storm moves buildings for free, and Children of the Nile places and demolishes for free. **Thebes:** a cheap move or full-refund "undo last build" for a few seconds, which matters on touch.
10. **History flavour at scenario start** (Nebuchadnezzar's per-mission history lessons) was liked by every reviewer who mentioned it. It is cheap to do.

## 8. How needs and tiers are communicated (cross-game)

| Game | Where the player sees it | What works | What fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anno 1800 | Residence panel: goods received, unmet needs; 5-state mood + rating; talking portraits | Split between "grow" needs and "mood" needs; advancing doesn't require happiness | Few tooltips, no glossary; ratios worked out by hand |
| Anno 117 | Category bars in the object menu; green = met, gold = exceeded; top-bar attribute hover lists sources | Partial credit; "any one food"; effects previewed while placing | Busy tabs; hard on gamepad |
| Timberborn | Top-left wellbeing number → per-need count of beavers satisfied; green highlights | "N of M satisfied" is fast to read; milestone bonuses are clear | Toolbar overload; thin tutorial |
| Farthest Frontier | Placement heat-map; overlay radius rings with % (red for bad); house bar tooltip listing each factor | Explains *why* a house won't rise | Thresholds are numeric and need guides |
| Manor Lords | Plot checklist with diamond ticks; hover upgrade button | Distance-aware checks | Met/unmet shown only by tan vs black diamonds |
| Banished | Town Hall tables; event log warnings at full and under 10% | One overview; target-relative alerts | Too many windows; "hard to get information" |
| Children of the Nile | Watch people; voice lines; 3 stat panels | Charm; the map carries state | Cramped panels; opaque bread economy |
| Nebuchadnezzar | House evolution; resource overview | Assigned routes beat random walkers | Silent devolution spiral; inaccurate overview |

**Implications for Thebes' house "next tier" checklist:**
- One row per requirement: icon + plain words + status in words ("met", "partly — 2 of 3 foods", "missing") + colour. Never colour or shape alone.
- Group the rows: "To fill this house" (basic) and "To rise to Tier N" (upgrade). Optionally a third: "Bonus" (luxuries → tax or mood).
- Use Anno 117's green/gold distinction for met versus more than met, so over-supply feels rewarded.
- Every missing row gets a "Show me" that pans to the nearest fix or to the build button, like Against the Storm's Build button inside the recipe panel.
- Coverage by reach along roads: preview the reach while placing (Anno 117 and Farthest Frontier both show effects at placement), and show "in reach" or "not in reach" per service row.
- Warn before a downgrade, with a countdown and its cause. Nebuchadnezzar's silent spiral is the anti-pattern.
- City-wide view: "N of M Tier-4 houses have beer", in the style of Timberborn, as one line per need in the People tab.

## 9. Recurring complaints worth designing out

1. **Information buried in sub-menus or nested radials** (Nebuchadnezzar; Anno 117 tabs; Anno 1800 console's four-step sawmill). Rule: any building within two taps of the build button. Any number within one tap of the top bar.
2. **Manual logistics that breaks when the city changes** (Nebuchadnezzar caravans). Keep goods routing automatic. The player's levers are roads, placement and "keep up to N" targets.
3. **Silent state changes:** houses devolving, systems switching on at thresholds, first droughts unannounced (Nebuchadnezzar, Ostriv, Timberborn). Always warn ahead and name the cause.
4. **Tutorials that stop early or hint late** (Timberborn, Anno 1800). Keep the goals strip running all game, and hint on stall signals.
5. **Long waits with nothing to do** (Children of the Nile; Nebuchadnezzar's slow gold; an Anno 117 newcomer "staring at the screen", via summary). Always offer a parallel goal. Speed controls plus a "next thing to do" pointer.
6. **Requirement states shown by colour or shape alone** (Manor Lords diamonds). Use words + icon + colour.
7. **Advice that names a fix but not its prerequisite** (Kingdoms and Castles tax adviser). Every hint links to the first actionable step.
8. **Stats that are wrong, or arrive late** (Nebuchadnezzar's inaccurate overview; Anno's statistics screen eight months after launch). Ship a simple, correct made/used figure per good from day one.
9. **Overwhelming build bars full of locked items** (Timberborn). Show only unlocked buildings.
10. **Icon-only UI** (Against the Storm had to add building names). Use labels under icons, in short plain English.
11. **Thin late game once stable** (Banished, Timberborn, Kingdoms and Castles). Milestones, monuments, caravans and later threats should keep goals coming.
12. **Efficient layouts that look samey** (Anno 1800). Desirability and decoration should reward variety, so good-looking cities also work well.
13. **A single gated money source early on** (Nebuchadnezzar trade-only gold). Taxes from the first houses. Trade as a later boost, not a lifeline.
14. **Controller and touch menus that navigate inconsistently** (Anno 1800 console, Against the Storm on Switch). One navigation model everywhere, a few large targets per screen, and adjustable text size.
