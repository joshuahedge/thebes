# Version 9.1 "First hours": rulings 1710-1724 (Track H, the heirs)

The habits are tests/v91/h/habits.js (`heirHabit`, `both`), taken by the balance runs' drivers through a new `adapt` option (tests/stage4/balance/capitalRun.js `runCapital`, tests/v50/grand/grandRun.js `runGrand`, tests/v60/balance/dangerRuns.js `lateDangerRun`, `greatDangerRun`, `grandDangerRun`; the late and great-work runs through their `onMonth`). The two words are src/sim/court.js `outputPerMonth` and src/ui/text.js's Track H section (`S46_RIVER_TEXT.nilometerNoteFor`, `FIRST_TEXT.first_nilometer.whyFor`, read by src/ui/s46/river.js). Tests: tests/v91/h/ (words, habits) and tests/v90/balance/runs.test.js (T1 for the Builder and T3 for the Unconquered, re-pinned with the habits). The other heirs' long runs are the probe below (ruling 1620's split), a scratch test never committed.

1710. The scripted players learn a habit per heir's weakness, reading the sheets a player reads; the Steady's runs are unchanged. The Steady has no habit, so a run with `adapt` and the Steady is the 9.0 run to the last number (a test compares the capital run's year-6 world, serialized, with and without it: Capital on day 600, 50,457 gold, lowest 255, score 360). — Cost if wrong: the scripts play heirs better than a new player does.
1711. The heirs are retuned by `TRAITS` data only, where an adaptive run still misses T1-T3. — Cost if wrong: a trait felt less than designed.
1712. The court's yearly due and the Nilometer's line read the heir. `outputPerMonth` (the court's reckoning of a month's output: request and gift sizes, the bumper harvest, an old save's first quota) is times production.js `heirRate`, so the Bountiful's farms count 15% more and the Merchant's workshops 15% less, as they make them; the Nilometer's sheet and its first say this city's Low flood, "40% becomes 60%" for the Bountiful (heirLoss, the sim's rule) and 9.0's "60% becomes 73%" for every other heir. How to play keeps the static line: it states the game's rule, not a city's. — Cost if wrong: none; the words match the sim.
1713. `outputPerMonth` does not read the flood. Reading it would change the size of every city's requests, gifts and bumper harvests, the Steady's too, which this release may not do (no sim rule changes but the lesson's and `TRAITS`) and which would move every Steady balance pin. The yearly quota already reads what the city really made (the year's snapshot), flood and heir included. — Cost if wrong: in a Low-flood year the court asks a farm town for a little more grain than its farms made that month.
1714. The Builder's habit (taxes x0.95): once the great temple is founded, the palace's High rate while the treasury holds under a month of Normal-rate taxes, back to Normal at two months; a palace is laid, on the free spot farthest from any home that no script claims, when there is none and the treasury holds it with 400 to spare. Measured first: from day 0 below three months, High at the capital run's day 600 changed the whole run and the great work was not won by year 20; from Capital below one month, the same; gated on the founding, the great-work run plays as 9.0's until year 16 and wins on day 6,235 (the Steady's 6,380: T1 met), never in debt (lowest 713). — Cost if wrong: a Builder player who never touches taxes still finishes 260 days late.
1715. The Unconquered's habit (first warning 100 days sooner): every warned raid is met with towers by the building each band strikes first (the late run's `meetWarning`), from the capital years on. With it the seed-5 dangers run beats all 13 raids (9.0: lost 6-12), seed 10 all 11, the late run all 10 and the late dangers run all 10 (T3 met); the great work is won on day 6,245 (T1 met, -135) and the year-6 gold is 49,794 (-1.3%, T2 met). No data change: `sooner` stays 100 (ruling 1626's 100 -> 15 is not needed). — Cost if wrong: an Unconquered player who builds towers only by the script's count still loses the seed-5 raids.
1716. The Merchant's habit (workshops x0.85): it keeps round(n / 0.85) of each workshop kind where the scripts laid n (a fourth potter by the three), each on the nearest free spot within 8 tiles of one of them that a road touches, no script claims, and has its input and a store for its goods in reach (else taken back, refunded), with the gold (400 to spare) and the workers to staff it. — Cost if wrong: the Merchant's runs still lose to a shortage the trait's line names.
1717. The Bountiful and the Generous get no habit. Their weaknesses never bite in the balance runs: no balance map has a floodplain (the capital map has no water, so a flood never touches its farms) and no script sets the High rate. Their runs differ from the Steady's only by their strengths, which move the thin-margin scripts (rulings 791, 1543, 1627). — Cost if wrong: their T1 and T3 misses stay unexplained by a habit.
1718. `adapt` is opt-in, off by default, so every older balance run and pin (the Steady's and 9.0's T4-T7 heir measures) plays as it did; tests/v90/balance/runs.test.js passes `adapt` and its T1 and T3 pins are re-set, each marked WAVE2-PIN. The habits never build on ground a script will want (the capital script's steps, the great-work run's temple plot and streets), so every script step still goes where it went. — Cost if wrong: none to the game.
1719. No `TRAITS` change in this release. The Builder's T2 (year-6 gold 37,278, -26.1%, NOT MET by 1.1 points: its habit waits for the great temple) was tried with taxes 0.95 -> 0.96: T2 met (-19.8%) but the great work moved to day 6,777 (T1 missed) and seed 10 lost 4 raids, so it is not kept.
