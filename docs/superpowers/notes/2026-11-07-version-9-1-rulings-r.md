# Version 9.1 "First hours": rulings 1675-1684 (Track R, never stuck)

The loading screen and the early error line (`index.html`'s two head blocks, `css/v91.css`), the global handler and the error card (`src/ui/v91/rescue.js`, wired by `src/boot.js`), and Back to gold (`src/ui/v91/debtSheet.js`, registered in `src/ui/createUI.js`). Words are `V91_R_TEXT` in `src/ui/text.js`; the debt alert's fix line (`alertText`'s 'debt' case) now reads it.

1675. A throw in a day's tick taints the city: the loop stops within that frame, the game pauses, the card shows, and autosave and the hooks' save are blocked for that world (the update's Restart and Home too, which then wait as for a failed save), so the slot keeps the last month's save and its backup. The card offers Reload from the save (the page reloads, to the title with Continue), Export this city and Copy the details, each with its consequence under it before the tap. — Cost if wrong: a city that was in fact sound is not saved until Reload (at most one game month lost).
1676. The loading screen is built by an inline script in the head (markup in the head would end it early) as soon as the body exists, from the page's own inline CSS, and boot removes it when the title shows or, with a development flag, when its game starts (`rescue.loadingDone`, which also calls `performance.mark('thebes-ready')` once, for Track P's profile). After 15 s it says "Still loading. Slow connection?" with Reload; a `nomodule` line says the browser is too old, with no Reload. — Cost if wrong: none; the screen is one element.
1677. The early error line is a capture-phase `error` listener (a failed script element's error does not bubble) and an `unhandledrejection` listener, active only while the loading screen is up: a module that fails to load, a syntax error or a start that throws is said on the loading screen with Reload. After the title shows it does nothing, and the rescue handler takes over. — Cost if wrong: a failure in a dynamic import after the title shows only the card catches.
1678. A throw in the draw is counted; three frames in a row stop the loop (a frame that drew resets the count, `rescue.frameOk`). The city is sound, so it is saved now and the card shows with Reload. Uncaught errors that arrive ten within a second are one failure that keeps coming (a throw in `onEvents` or the frame gate repeats every frame) and are treated the same way. — Cost if wrong: ten distinct handler errors in a second would stop a healthy game (the card says the city was saved).
1679. A throw in a handler, a timer or a promise gives one toast a session, "That didn't work; your city is safe", and the game goes on; later errors only add to the details. Not failures of the game and so ignored: the browser's ResizeObserver notice, `AbortError` (a cancelled share), `NotAllowedError` (a blocked sound), the cross-origin "Script error." and a rejection with no reason. Before the title, an error is said on the loading screen if it is still up, else the card shows (Reload and Copy only). — Cost if wrong: one of those filtered errors was real and went unseen.
1680. One card a session. It is a sheet at the screen's edge (the bottom on a phone, the bottom corner on a wide screen), Tab stays inside it and Esc does nothing: the way out is Reload. Copy the details is the version, the build stamp, where it threw, the device's user agent and each error's first lines (name, message, three frames), with no address, query or hash; when the clipboard refuses, the text is shown selected. The dev hook `?crash=tick|render|ui` is read once at boot in `rescue.js` and nowhere else: `tick` puts a getter on the world's buildings that throws once, only when `tickDay` reads them (the first day after Play); `render` throws three frames in a row; `ui` throws once from a timer 1.5 s after the game starts. — Cost if wrong: none for a player; the flag is for tests and Josh's real-phone check.
1681. The debt alert's View, wherever it is tapped (the banner, the Messages tray, the alert list, Welcome back), opens Back to gold. Its fix line reads "Pause what costs most; View shows which, and the ways out"; City advice's treasury item reads the same line. — Cost if wrong: that advice item points at a View it does not have when the treasury is not yet below 0.
1682. Back to gold lists this month's taxes, wages, upkeep and soldiers from `monthFigures`, then the three buildings that cost most (upkeep plus its staff's wages as `monthFigures` charges them, never one Pause would be refused or one already paused), each with Pause (`setPaused`, then the labour reassigned). A row says "saves N" only while no level of work is short of workers (`world.labour.shortLevels` empty), because then the workers freed have no job to go to; else "saves up to N: its workers may take other jobs". — Cost if wrong: with workers short the month gains less than the row's cost, and the row said "up to".
1683. The months are this month's balance run forward: months to clear a debt is `ceil(debt / net)` when net is above 0 and none otherwise; the room before `ECONOMY.debtFloor` (-500, where `canAfford` refuses every purchase) is `gold + 500`, and at a falling balance its months are `floor(room / -net)`. Trade, court dues and events are not in them ("at this rate"). — Cost if wrong: a caravan or a court due moves the real month.
1684. The levers are listed only while the sim honours them. The tax lever needs a palace and is one step up from its rate (Low to Normal, Normal to High), worth exactly `taxesAt` of the step less today's taxes, with the mood it costs (-8). The sale lever needs an open route whose caravan or boat will land (`arrivalAt`) and is the best household good or luxury a partner buys (never food, which the homes eat, nor raw goods, which workshops use), above the homes' use for three months rounded up to the Trade sheet's step of 10 and what the temple and court are owed, up to the caravan's quarter of the yearly quota, worth at least 10 gold; it says "Set X to Sell with Keep at least N", and with that order the next caravan pays exactly the gold shown. Each has a Show me (the palace's sheet; the Trade sheet). — Cost if wrong: a surplus the player would rather keep is offered; the Trade sheet's own Keep at least is the control.
