# Thebes v10 review: the refund window and the look (11 Nov 2026, for Josh)

**Where the game is:** 9.3.1 "Easier on a phone" is live (main = origin, 3582196). Nothing is unpushed. Every item below was checked today against that build: a fresh walk of the title, lesson and skip flow at 360 to 390 px, headless screenshots at 390x844 (DPR 2), 1280x800 and 1920x1080, the camera and clock code, and the follow-ups notes from 9.1 to 9.3.1 (the still-open inventory is section 4).

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The first-hours work of 9.1 and 9.2 closed most of the refund lens: the lesson no longer burns its own granary, the hand-over is honest, alerts roll up, there is a loading screen, an error card and an About. What remains is of a different kind: nothing has been run on a real phone or on the store target, the first thing a new player sees is half a screen of empty tan, the desktop interface is phone-sized on a 1080p monitor, the store wrapper does not exist, and performance has gone backwards since 9.3. Those are the v10 items. The art itself is the game's strongest asset and needs polish, not change.

## 1. What a refund window is for this game

- **Steam:** any reason, within 14 days of purchase and under 2 hours of play. **Google Play:** 48 hours, no play limit. The web version stays free (your call from 6.0), so a buyer has paid for convenience, a wrapper and goodwill, not for content they cannot get elsewhere.
- **How much game fits in 2 hours.** A month is 30 s at 1x and the speeds are 1x, 2x and 3x (`src/sim/time.js`, `src/ui/speed.js`). The lesson is 10 to 15 min. A year is then 6 min at 1x and 2 min at 3x. The scripted great-work win lands near year 19 to 20 (6.0 follow-ups), so a confident player at 3x sees the whole arc inside the window; a careful first-timer at 1x reaches Town or Market town. Two consequences:
  - the first 20 minutes after the lesson must show the player what the game *is* (a goal they chose, a rank arriving, something built that changed the city), not only what to tap;
  - the price has to match a 3-to-6 hour game or the reviews say "short". That is a store-page and price decision (section 5), not a code one.
- **The three refund reasons that matter most, in order:** "it doesn't run or looks broken on my machine" (a store build's commonest), "I didn't know what to do or it was boring", "it isn't what the page promised". Everything in section 2 is ranked against those.

## 3. The look: what is strong and what to improve

**Strong, keep:** the art direction holds at every size I captured. The whitewashed stacked homes with lavender shadows, the teal-painted temples, the papyrus interface with the frieze band and ankh, the cartouche-shaped title and sheet headings, the one-panel-at-a-time rule. The desktop sample at 1280 and 1920 reads as a finished game. The lesson card is clear, well spaced and well typed.

## 5. Proposed v10 shape: two releases

**10.0 "Store-ready"** (the refund window). In this order, because each gate protects the next:
1. Your real-phone pass (R1, R9, L6) with the 9.3 checklist; findings become the first fix batch.
2. First view and look: camera start and Show me frames keep the map on screen; the off-map area drawn (R3, L1); scribe bubble in the phone budget (R6); the C-item batch (R8); button language (L3); foot shadows (L4); drawn dock icons (L2).
3. Desktop: default UI scale by width, top-bar date (R5, L5); a 1920 and 2560 screenshot test.
4. Performance track with a failing budget test (R4).
5. The Electron shell (R2): fullscreen default, window state remembered, F11 and Esc, quit saves, platform flag, the Install button and PWA notices off, version label 1.0 with the build sha behind About, saves in the app's own user-data folder with Export and Import kept. Prove it on a clean Windows machine or a fresh user account, not your dev machine.
6. Store-page set (L7) and the one-line pitch on the page matching the title card's sentence, so the page promises exactly what the first minute shows.

**10.1 "Worth the price"** (hours 3 to 6): the lesson's decision step (R7), middle monuments shortened (L2), the four heirs retuned so each can win (L1), the Capital alert count (L3), decree bars played once each (L6). Google Play after 10.1, since Capacitor needs Node and Android Studio and a 12-tester closed test, and the phone pass will already have told us what Android needs.

**Not in v10:** eras, neighbours, the oracle, colour-blind palettes (unless you reopen it), moving buildings, rotate view.

## 6. Decisions only you can make

1. **Which store first.** Steam Windows (the plan) makes R2 and R5 the gates and the desktop look the priority; Google Play first makes R1 and R4 the gates. The order above assumes Steam first.
2. **Price band.** A 3-to-6 hour game with a free web twin sits at the low end (the range most small city builders launch at). The page should say the web version exists; hiding it is the surest "not what I expected" review.
3. **Version on the store.** 1.0 outside, 10.0 inside (About shows both), or carry 10.0 outside. Recommended: 1.0 outside.
4. **Colour-blind.** Stays dropped unless you say otherwise.
5. **The free web version at launch.** Keep it (your call so far), or hold it a month behind the store build. Keep it; it is the demo.
